# Targeted decoder literature matrix

This is a focused feasibility review, not a systematic review. Metrics are deliberately not numerically compared unless code, noise, distance, syndrome regime, and denominator match. Primary papers, original preprints, and official software sources are prioritized.

| Method | Verifiable source | Code / noise / metric reported in source | Complexity and assumptions | Applicability to Q-SHIFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact maximum-likelihood and tensor-network decoding | Bravyi, Suchara & Vargo, *Efficient Algorithms for Maximum Likelihood Decoding in the Surface Code*, PRA 90 (2014), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.90.032326), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4883) | 2D surface code with noiseless syndrome in studied setup; exact MLD for a restricted independent bit/phase noise model; approximate MPS contraction for more general noise. Reported logical error improvement over MWPM on that paper's own model. | Exact restricted construction is special-noise dependent; MPS cost grows with bond dimension (paper gives O(nχ³)). | Strong conceptual reference; current circuit-level categorical bursts and repeated noisy measurements need a different factorization and could increase contraction width. Existing toy MAP is more immediately verifiable. |
| General-noise tensor-network decoding | Darmawan & Poulin, *Linear-time general decoding algorithm for the surface code*, PR E 97, 051302 (2018), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.97.051302), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01879) | Surface-code model including coherent and spatially correlated noise; approximate logical-channel calculation. | Tensor-network approximate contraction; resource depends on truncation and network structure. | Relevant to correlated noise, but not drop-in exact reference for Q-SHIFT's Stim circuit. |
| Belief propagation plus matching / belief-find | Higgott et al., *Improved Decoding of Circuit Noise and Fragile Boundaries of Tailored Surface Codes*, PRX 13, 031007 (2023), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.13.031007), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04948) | Surface-code circuit-noise decoding; BP uses full hyperedge model then MWPM or union-find returns a syndrome-consistent correction. | Efficient message passing followed by graph decoding; BP alone can fail convergence or valid-syndrome requirements. | Natural later baseline if factor/hypergraph representation retains categorical correlations; requires fair tuning/runtime and same DEM semantics. |
| Correlated matching | PyMatching v2 official API and source: [API](https://pymatching.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api.html), [source](https://github.com/oscarhiggott/PyMatching/blob/master/src/pymatching/matching.py); Fowler, *Minimum weight perfect matching of fault-tolerant quantum error correction in the presence of correlations*, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.0863) | Two-pass matching can use decomposable higher-order error mechanisms such as a surface-code Y error; requires construction from circuit/DEM and explicit decode flag. | Restricted to correlations represented through valid decomposition hints; does not solve arbitrary hypergraph MAP. | Directly available and already used in newer pilots. Keep flag on/off as a controlled ablation; do not equate it with adding an omitted burst edge/probability. |
| BP+OSD for quantum LDPC codes | Panteleev & Kalachev, *Degenerate Quantum LDPC Codes With Good Finite Length Performance*, Quantum 5, 585 (2021), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2021-11-22-585); see also original decoding papers cited in that work | Broad quantum LDPC performance; often benchmarked on codeword/logical failure rates under specified Pauli channels. | BP plus ordered-statistics search repairs degeneracy/nonconvergence at additional combinatorial cost; OSD order and iteration budget matter. | Valuable if Q-SHIFT later targets general LDPC/hypergraph models. For this tiny surface memory, first compare against simpler exact toy MAP and optimized matching. |
| Graph neural-network decoding | Lange et al., *Data-driven decoding of quantum error correcting codes using graph neural networks*, PR Research 7, 023181 (2025), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.7.023181), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01241) | Simulated circuit-level surface-code data and real repetition-code data; source reports GNN performance competitive with/outperforming matching for selected conditions. | Training is data intensive; graph model consumes richer detector context; generalization depends on code/noise and train/test separation. | Could capture cross-detector structure beyond Q-SHIFT's scalar profile, but existing local CNN/MLP diagnostics underperform the matching baseline. Defer until residual headroom is demonstrated. |
| Recurrent transformer decoder | Bausch et al., *Learning high-accuracy error decoding for quantum processors*, Nature 635 (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08148-8), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05900) | Surface-code decoding on Google processor data, including d=3 and d=5; learned temporal decoder state and final logical prediction. | Significant data/training/model complexity; deployment result tied to hardware and architecture. | Relevant to temporal structure, but its published accuracy is not comparable to this synthetic 40-detector benchmark. It does not establish that a transformer helps here. |
| Scalable neural decoder | Chamberland et al., *Scalable Neural Decoder for Topological Surface Codes*, PRL 128 (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.080505) | Topological surface codes under depolarizing and syndrome-measurement noise; source emphasizes scalability and decoder runtime. | Local/window decomposition and training; performance depends on code/noise model. | Potential distance-scaling candidate only after Q-SHIFT's hard-coded distance-3 detector geometry is generalized. |
| Data-driven temporal noise adaptation | Spitz et al., *Adaptive Weight Estimator for QEC in a Time-Dependent Environment*, Advanced Quantum Technologies 1 (2018), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/qute.201800012), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02360); Huo & Li, NJP 19, 123032 (2017), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/aa916e) | Syndrome/error-statistics-based matching weight estimation under time-dependent noise. | Window size trades estimator variance against tracking lag; too-fast drift can outrun calibration. | Direct prior art: Q-SHIFT must compare policy and operating envelope, not claim first adaptation. |
| Drift and correlated graph reweighting | Wang et al., *DGR: Tackling Drifted and Correlated Noise in QEC via Decoding Graph Re-weighting*, [arXiv:2311.16214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16214) | Simulations of drifted and correlated noise with edge/edge-pair statistics from matching outcomes. | Depends on reliable graph statistics and inference from decoder solutions; costs and setting differ from Q-SHIFT's detector profile estimator. | Direct comparator/novelty boundary for online reweighting. |
| Sliding-window drift estimation | Bhardwaj et al., *Adaptive Estimation of Drifting Noise in QEC*, PRX Quantum 7, 033024 (2026), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1hc-nqw5), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09491) | Syndrome-only Pauli-noise drift estimation; paper reports frequency/window analyses and simulation under phenomenological and circuit-level models. | Window/filter trade-off and iterative/overlapping window tracking; assumptions on model family and observability matter. | Very close prior art. A Q-SHIFT contribution needs equal-budget comparisons to trailing/EWMA/periodic policies and explicit mismatch. |
| Hardware decoder-prior optimization | Sivak, Newman & Klimov, *Optimization of Decoder Priors for Accurate QEC*, PRL 133, 150603 (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.150603) | Hardware memory experiments; prior optimization targets logical decoding performance. | Calibration objective and hardware conditions are specific; optimizing decoder utility need not identify physical rates. | Motivates comparing decoder performance directly rather than estimator RMSE alone. |
| Hardware control and decoder adaptation | Sivak et al., *Reinforcement learning control of quantum error correction*, Nature 655 (2026), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10759-2), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08493) | Willow processor; adapts system control/decoder steering using error-detection feedback. | Hardware-control loop is broader than software-only graph weight updating. | Rules out broad “first adaptive QEC” claims; not an apples-to-apples baseline for the synthetic simulator. |
| Syndrome-only identifiability | Wagner et al., *Pauli channels can be estimated from syndrome measurements in QEC*, Quantum 6, 809 (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2022-09-19-809), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14252) | Identifiability under structural constraints on Pauli-channel correlations, related to pure distance. | Finite sample conditioning and arbitrary circuit-level noise are not guaranteed by the theorem. | Supports a careful identifiability screen before adding parameters to the online estimator. |

## Novelty judgment

The broad ingredients—matching, correlations, syndrome-only parameter estimation, sliding windows, online decoder update, residual neural decoding, and graph neural decoding—are established. A defensible Q-SHIFT result would be an empirically careful operating-envelope study, or a mathematically justified detector-to-decoder decision rule, that compares policies under equal information and compute and includes measurement-model mismatch. No percentage can establish novelty.
