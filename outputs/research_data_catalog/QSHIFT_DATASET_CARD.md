# Q-SHIFT Research Dataset Card

**Version:** development evidence inventory, 2026-11-07
**Project:** simulation-based quantum error-correction research using Stim and PyMatching
**Data status:** locally retained artifacts vary by phase; this card does not authorize access to sealed independent-test material.

## Purpose and scope

Q-SHIFT studies decoding of simulated surface-code detector records and logical observables under stationary and synthetic changing-noise conditions. The datasets are generated by software models, not collected from quantum hardware. They support paired decoder comparisons, estimator development, and reproducibility audits.

## Population and representation

Most records represent batches of detector bits and one or more observable bits from rotated surface-code memory circuits. Some neural experiments also store model logits, probabilities, predictions, checkpoints, or training state. Exact circuit, partition, seed, and array schemas are experiment-specific and are documented in each phase's configuration and audit files. Stage 4C's verified evaluation records contain 40 detectors, one observable, and paired predictions for five decoder arms.

## Noise and labels

Noise families include categorical gate-level Pauli channels, measurement errors, and a synthetic spatial common-cause event that applies `X8 X10` independently at round boundaries. The latter is an interpretable simulator proxy, not a measured hardware process. Logical-observable labels come from Stim's configured observables. The Stage 4C estimator receives only detector calibration batches; labels are used after prediction for scoring. See individual protocols for exact fault probabilities and DEM approximation settings.

## Provenance, splits, and audit status

Each experiment has its own seed inventory and partition definitions. The strongest available studies have independent audits that recompute predictions and metrics from saved arrays. Historical controller failures and protocol deviations remain documented; an audit recovery does not change original run status. The independent-test allocation is outside this catalog's inspection scope and remains sealed.

## Retention and limitations

Raw arrays and model weights are retained locally where available and generally excluded from Git. Lightweight summaries, protocols, source, seed metadata, and hashes are intended for version control. Some earlier experiments retain only reports/checkpoints or have incomplete calibration arrays. Phase 4C's run directory has 44 local raw evidence files; their hashes are indexed by its evidence manifest, while large arrays are ignored by Git. No single cross-project deduplicated row count has been established.

## Intended uses

Appropriate uses include reproducing the documented simulator experiments, teaching, and development comparisons. Inappropriate uses without additional evidence include estimating real-device performance, claiming universal decoder superiority, or treating synthetic burst probabilities as physical hardware rates.

## Ethical and safety notes

This project uses simulated data and contains no human-subject or personal data. Local run directories may contain large experimental data and must remain governed by the repository's `.gitignore`. Do not export or inspect sealed independent-test material.

## Known limitations

- Counts below are per-study allocations as reported by each study, not a globally deduplicated total.
- Some studies may reuse templates, code, or derived calibration profiles; this does not establish row-level identity across all historical runs.
- RNG collision screens cover recoverable seed metadata only and cannot rule out undocumented RNG use.
- Stim DEM decomposition can approximate disjoint categorical mechanisms; see the phase-specific limitations.
- No hardware integration or real-time device demonstration is established.
