## Decoder theory, matching, and correlations

**Maximum likelihood and degeneracy.** Bravyi, Suchara, and Vargo study exact maximum-likelihood decoding under restricted surface-code noise assumptions and approximate tensor-network decoding for broader local noise. The distinction matters because the posterior probability of a logical class sums over all faults in that class, while minimum-weight matching selects a correction under a graph model. Their result establishes a promising theoretical family, not a claim that the full Q-SHIFT circuit can be decoded exactly at affordable cost. Q-SHIFT's categorical feasibility note counts 209 independent fault locations and 2^609 complete assignments, while exact exhaustive reference checks cover only small toys. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.90.032326)

**BP+OSD.** Roffe and coauthors describe belief propagation with ordered-statistics postprocessing for quantum LDPC decoding. This is established prior art for combining approximate marginal inference with a more structured search. The expedition brief states that Stage S found min-sum 0.9 worse and OSD_CS-40 plus BP-200 near neutral on a 1.2 million-shot development pool. I could not find the Stage S records on the published `main` checkout, and I did not inspect Claude's branch. Those reported figures remain user-provided context here, not independently verified evidence. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.043423)

**Belief-assisted and correlated matching.** Higgott and coauthors' belief-matching and belief-find work reports improvements over ordinary MWPM for the studied circuit-noise/tailored-code models. PyMatching v2.4.0 official documentation describes its correlated mode as two passes of sparse blossom, with correlation reweighting informed by the first matching, and requires enabling the feature both at model construction and decode time. These are established approaches and make a “correlation-aware matching is new” claim untenable. Q-SHIFT's own matched 720,000-shot development pilot found 3,349 versus 3,532 pooled-fixed errors for correlation on/off, but missed its predeclared practical-effect gate and had adverse cells. These are bounded simulation results on one distance-3 categorical Pauli setup. [PRX DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.13.031007) [PyMatching v2.4.0 README](https://github.com/oscarhiggott/PyMatching/blob/v2.4.0/README.md) [PyMatching paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3505637)

**Graph representation and Stim semantics.** Stim v1.16.0 documentation says DEM error mechanisms are required to be independent and gives mutually exclusive custom Pauli channels as an example that cannot be represented exactly, requiring approximation during conversion. The DEM-generation API refuses the Phase 2B `PAULI_CHANNEL_2` circuits unless `approximate_disjoint_errors=True`, as confirmed by a deterministic check on all six conditions. Therefore a matched “exact categorical DEM versus approximated DEM” experiment cannot be implemented using this conversion route. A valid native categorical decoder must retain mutually exclusive gate outcomes and separately validate its detector/observable maps. [Stim v1.16.0 CLI guide](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim/blob/v1.16.0/doc/usage_command_line.md) [Stim v1.16.0 DEM format](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim/blob/v1.16.0/doc/file_format_dem_detector_error_model.md)

## Noise adaptation, ensembles, and selective computation

**Noise-aware adaptation.** Darmawan's 2025 tensor-network study asks which local noise parameters materially affect near-optimal surface-code decoding. It establishes that decoder adaptation to local noise is an active studied question. The local review identifies a possible narrower contribution in testing whether a practical calibration-selected PyMatching bank has enough out-of-root utility headroom for causal switching. Q-SHIFT's Phase 4C Stage 2 effect was +0.00001556 calibrated-minus-pooled LER, with 95% interval [-0.00004889, +0.00007667] and p=0.66658, while calibration traces are missing. This does not support a current adaptive advantage. [APS DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/r2dc-qcrx)

**Syndrome-conditioned ensembles.** Sheth, Jafarzadeh, and Gheorghiu study neural selection among topological-code decoders using syndrome features and decoder success labels. Q-SHIFT's own uncertainty-aware selection analysis reported weak out-of-root utility prediction, with AUC 0.570 for calibration utility and 0.464 for current-syndrome utility. Broad per-syndrome decoder selection is therefore not a novelty direction, and local Q-SHIFT evidence is negative. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.101.032338)

**Selective acceptance and adaptive windows.** Smith, Brown, and Bartlett analyze decoding ambiguity and selective rejection. Two relevant 2026 preprints in the local novelty audit, Mishima et al.'s complementary-gap adaptive-window decoder and Oberoi, Viszlai, and Chong's ADaPT, route low-confidence syndromes to a more expensive larger-window pass. Another local source identifies Choudhary et al.'s confidence-gated meta-decoder. They are directly relevant prior art for confidence-triggered escalation. The Q-SHIFT local review reports these as preprints, not peer-reviewed consensus. The present execution could not retrieve the arXiv records independently. [Smith et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-024-01883-4) [Mishima et al. arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14637) [ADaPT arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01149) [MDQEC-QAS arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10707)

**Hardware adaptation and calibration.** Chen et al. report calibrated matching from measured correlated events. Ziad et al. present an FPGA local-clustering decoder with an adaptation engine driven by prelearned runtime controls such as heralded leakage. These results reinforce that calibration and runtime adaptation need a clear observable trigger and hardware/resource model. Q-SHIFT's simulator regime labels are not hardware-observable device controls. [Chen et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.110504) [LCD DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66773-x)

## Neural methods and code co-design

**Neural decoders.** Baireuther et al. study machine-learning correction of correlated phase errors, and Sheth et al. study decoder selection. The 2024 AlphaQubit article is a strong evidence benchmark, combining simulated and experimental training and evaluating experimental surface-code data at distances 3 and 5, with simulated results reported to distance 11. Q-SHIFT's position-preserving CNN was better than its earlier pooled CNN on small development roots but remained worse than fixed PyMatching. A neural model is not equivalent to exact logical-class inference, and large training performance cannot be assumed to transfer to Q-SHIFT's circuit. [Baireuther et al. Quantum DOI](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2018-07-26-48) [AlphaQubit DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08148-8)

**Biased-noise codes.** Tuckett et al.'s XZZX/tailored surface-code work demonstrates that changing code structure and boundaries can exploit highly biased noise. This is a code-and-resource comparison, not a decoder-only gain from switching matching weights. Q-SHIFT's 10:1:1 Pauli composition is synthetic and has no hardware-calibrated provenance. [PRX DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.9.041031)

**Distance and operating limits.** Dennis et al. establish the statistical-mechanical threshold framework for topological memories; modern hardware papers demonstrate distance-dependent suppression under specific device and circuit models. Q-SHIFT's headroom runs at d=3,5,7 were designed to screen specialized decoder headroom, not estimate a general threshold. A future operating map should be labelled finite-distance simulation evidence unless it includes enough distances, noise points, and finite-size analysis. [Dennis et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1499754) [Google 2023 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05434-1) [Google 2025 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08449-y)

## Rare-event statistics and inference limits

For a target LER p and zero observed failures in N IID trials, the one-sided exact binomial upper confidence bound is `1 - alpha^(1/N)`. The expedition's script computes the sample size needed for a prespecified candidate-rate ceiling. These counts are optimistic when noise roots are heterogeneous, decoder/model selection is performed, or shots are clustered. Proctor et al.'s quantum-computer benchmarking review supports matching metrics and uncertainty to the claim; Lakens explains why a nonsignificant difference is not an equivalence result without a prespecified smallest effect and adequate precision. No Q-SHIFT importance-sampling method was found in the reviewed local assets. Importance sampling remains an untested estimator direction and requires weight-variance and support checks. [Proctor et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42254-024-00796-z) [Lakens DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177)

## Research gaps after overlap review

1. Local categorical exact inference has only toy validation; the full d=3 model's exhaustive space is intractable. A structured categorical factor graph could be studied, but the new loopy sum-product screen in this expedition was inaccurate on one exact toy and is stopped.
2. A precise mechanism behind the correlation-mode heterogeneity is not identified; the planned exact-vs-approximate DEM comparison is blocked by Stim's categorical-channel semantics.
3. Stage S's detailed BP+OSD source and records are absent from the published `main` worktree. The Stage S outcome listed above is context from the expedition prompt only.
4. New 2026 literature sources were described in local reviews, but current network restrictions prevented direct retrieval. No novelty claim should be based on this partial retrieval.

**Tensor-network decoding update.** The installed qecsim 1.0b9 `PlanarMPSDecoder` source describes the decoder as contracting stabilizer coset probabilities and cites Bravyi, Suchara, and Vargo's exact/approximate surface-code maximum-likelihood work (arXiv:1405.4883) and later tensor-network decoder studies (arXiv:1708.08474, arXiv:1812.08186). Its documentation explicitly says `chi=None` is exact up to rounding but exponentially slow in lattice size, while finite χ truncates each contraction and returns the most probable logical coset. This confirms bounded-χ planar MPS is established prior art. This execution could inspect qecsim's pinned local source but could not independently retrieve the cited papers because arXiv/DOI access was blocked; no novelty claim is made. The new χ=16 experiment is a model-specific feasibility result, not a novel decoding algorithm.

### Sequential calibration methods

Wald's sequential probability ratio test is classical methodology, not a new decoder contribution: Abraham Wald, *Sequential Analysis* (Wiley, 1947). The local implementation applies a Gaussian score likelihood model to a frozen four-feature detector-only classifier. The current experiment covers only two fixed synthetic profiles; it does not test change-point detection or model-misspecification robustness.

### Syndrome-round scheduling context

Changing the number of repeated syndrome-extraction rounds is a code/circuit resource choice within established spacetime surface-code decoding, not a new decoder. The local round-count work explicitly separates active entangling/measurement cycles from added data-idle time and compares decoder models built from each schedule. Its d3 result does not extend to d5 under the tested model, so no scaling or publication claim follows. The broader threshold and spacetime-memory context remains the established statistical-mechanical framework cited above (Dennis et al.); this update does not add a new literature claim.
