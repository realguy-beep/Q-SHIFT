# Teacher summary: adaptive and stronger fixed decoders

Q-SHIFT uses computer simulation to study how a quantum error-correction decoder behaves when errors change. In an earlier frozen Stage 5 simulation, adaptation had a 21.9% lower error rate than a fixed decoder designed for zero burst noise. That number describes that specific comparison and simulator. A later development study asked a stricter question: can adaptation improve on one fixed decoder calibrated to the average burst level?

The new study compared five methods on the same 1.5 million evaluation samples. The average adaptive error rate across four burst settings was about **0.0029 percentage points lower** than the pooled fixed decoder. This difference is far smaller than the project's predeclared 0.02 percentage-point practical benchmark, and the study is exploratory rather than confirmatory. The pooled decoder made more errors when burst noise was zero. A safety rule rejected the selected fixed setting on validation and fell back to the zero-burst decoder; that safe fallback then lost much of the burst-condition benefit.

The first version of the pooled calibration code used known simulated settings contrary to its written method. The team preserved that output and separately reconstructed the intended detector-only estimate from saved data. The reconstruction passed an independent numerical check, but the deviation means this was not a clean prospective execution. The result supports continued careful testing, not a claim that adaptation is superior. All work is simulation-only; no quantum hardware is controlled.
