# Which approach is worth building first?

The speed column describes the causal target. The logical-loss column is a mechanism or an uncertainty, not a predicted gain. Cost indicates relative development and paired validation effort, with every arm evaluated on the same sampled rows. See [literature assumptions](LITERATURE_REVIEW.md), [historical findings](RESPONSE_GAP_ROOT_CAUSE.md) and [study budget](PREREGISTERED_VALIDATION_PLAN.md).

| Priority and method | Response target | Logical-loss rationale | False switch risk | Existing simulator | Online compute | Novelty and reproducibility | Build and study cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Directional diagnosis + bounded decoder bank + risk gate | First usable next block for a representative state, otherwise abstain | Tests whether reliable earlier action recovers ON losses; first unseen model has no ready key | Weak ON, benign background changes and near-aliases require calibrated winner margin and reject region | Natural extension of AD3 recall; same features and budgets for controls | Low scoring and key selection; bank memory and build cost bounded separately | High auditability; novelty is tested application and decomposition, not the statistic | Moderate build and development calibration, shared paired evaluation |
| 2. Bounded short/long score plus AD3 or static fallback | Earlier evidence when class contrast is adequate; full estimate stays slow | Tests simpler fallback and whether current guard rather than raw learner causes delay | Lower actuation frequency with fallback; false rejects can remain costly | Direct; training new short-window guard is separate | Low for summary updates; fitting can dominate | High reproducibility; strong ablation and baseline | Low to moderate calibration and build |
| 3. Development-selected robust static and risk-aware hold | Constant action has no estimator lag and no ability to follow a regime | Could avoid false-switch and nominal-fallback costs; may sacrifice gains on recurring ON | None for fixed; staleness for hold | Direct and essential as comparator; same BP+OSD engine | Low update cost, one static decoder plus optional last-good key | Highest reproducibility; low algorithmic novelty | Small calibration grid and paired scoring |
| 4. Directional CUSUM/SR or window-limited GLR without bank | Earlier alarms for persistent changes with calibrated scores | Improves evidence only. Needs novel model or safe fallback to change loss | Composite pre-state and multiple starts; transient misses unless tuned | Feasible in streaming summaries | Low CUSUM/SR, moderate to high GLR fitting | Established formulas and explicit control | Low to moderate build, full matching-tail calibration |
| 5. Finite-state HMM/BOCPD with risk-aware action | Same causal floor, potential pooling for ambiguous sequences | Can combine return evidence with uncertain onset; depends on duration/emission fidelity | Can misclassify or stay stuck under unexpected dwell/background | Feasible for a bounded state bank | Small HMM cheap, BOCPD memory caps or particles need profiling | Reproducible if priors are frozen; recurrence contribution testable | Moderate to high prior, likelihood and failure testing |
| 6. Ordered prefix control with joint likelihood or sufficient feature subset | Target within-block action after a supported observed prefix | Could recover some loss before next block; short pulse may already be over | More looks, lower per-look evidence and timing races | Requires redesigned observation/evaluation schedule and a paired cadence control | More frequent decisions and prebuild requirements, independent of inference engine | Strong applied contribution if causality and net benefit are verified | High validation cost; separate stage after block-level pass |
| 7. Full posterior mixture or continuous model with particles/TN | Same information floor with smoother uncertainty response | Could help when detector states are ambiguous and logical posteriors can be calibrated | Posterior miscalibration may shift loss across states | Needs new posterior representation and matched model fidelity | High, potentially several decodes per batch | Scientifically interesting, lower reproducibility until approximation and compute are pinned | High build and evaluation cost |
| 8. Hazard/GP/neural forecasts | Earlier only if available pre-onset signals or a validated duration prior | Possible under reproducible precursors/drift; no expected gain on unannounced random steps | Hidden schedule shortcuts, prior mismatch and unnecessary preemptive switches | Needs new schedule families and exposure logs | Varies from cheap persistence to expensive training | Low confidence of transfer; higher value only after proving forecast signal | High training and out-of-family evaluation cost |

**Primary.** Priority 1. Compare with a matched-information simple score or short/long selector and actual AD3 replay logic on fresh development episodes.

**Conservative alternative.** Priority 3. Use the pooled strong static if neither conditional state recognition nor expected action benefit meets the gate. Treat last accepted as a comparator with a separately validated hold duration, especially for mixed or unknown states.

**Ambitious direction.** Priority 6. Move observation cadence first, then prove whether benefits survive joint-law validation, repeated-look safety and every-scored-row accounting. Use priority 5 as a modelling challenger only if simple directional scores leave documented information unused.
