# Methods for quicker reaction to noise changes

The relevant decision has three dates: evidence of a change, actionable identification of a model, and activation of a decoder. Classical detection papers mainly address the first date. Adaptive QEC papers validate particular noise models and decoding settings. Neither literature measures AD2/AD3's full loop. Links point to the primary references in the [evidence index](REFERENCES.md).

## A. Sequential detection

| Method | Principle and strongest transferable evidence | Assumption, difficulty and Q-SHIFT role |
|---|---|---|
| Page CUSUM | Recursively accumulate the positive running log likelihood evidence, \(S_t=\max(0,S_{t-1}+\ell_t)\). Moustakides gives exact minimax optimality in the classical specified-distribution setting at matched ARL. [E1](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Low cost with a scalar increment. Known pre/post feature laws or fitted scores required. A fitted single-state score loses robustness to nuisance change. A strong baseline and practical gate if thresholds are calibrated with actual resets. |
| Sequential GLR | Search recent onset indices and candidate post parameters for the maximum likelihood contrast. Window-limited versions bound cost and some attain asymptotic criteria under specific laws. [E2](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Medium to high cost with full joint laws. With marginal pseudo-likelihood and repeated overlapping looks the nominal likelihood calibration does not apply. Useful as the unknown-state detection/fit comparator, with finite window and trained nuisance model. |
| SPRT | Two boundaries on accumulated evidence give accept/reject for a defined pair and start. Wald–Wolfowitz establishes expected sample optimality for two simple laws at fixed errors. [E2](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Cheap for OFF versus one live candidate when tested after a prespecified start; restart and selection require their own operating-characteristic calibration. Suitable as a follow-up verification rule. |
| Shiryaev–Roberts | \((1+R_{t-1})\exp(\ell_t)\) sums likelihoods over possible starts. Published optimality depends on criterion, initialization and assumptions. [E3](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Low cost per specified alternative and sensitive to null mismatch/headstart. Compare if persistent changes are the main objective. ARL and short-window false switch risk differ. |
| Multi-hypothesis diagnosis | Compare each alternative against the null and plausible competitors, with separate stopping and identification criteria. [E3](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Natural for a small model bank and distinction between ON and OFF. Pairwise comparisons can grow quadratically. Include an unknown option and calibrate max score and wrong-model margin together. Best immediate fit to AD3's detection/identification separation. |
| E-factor and e-detector | Conditional null expectation bounded by one permits sequential evidence aggregation. E-detectors combine starts and can control ARL under stated assumptions. [E8](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Moderate update cost and high model-validation cost. Conditional validity for correlated, selected, recurrent regimes and finite-horizon error spending would need proof. Prior project work leaves d=5 applicability open. [Q9](REFERENCES.md#q-shift-evidence) |

## B. Bayesian filtering

| Method | Principle and evidence | Assumption, difficulty and Q-SHIFT role |
|---|---|---|
| BOCPD | Forward posterior on run length, with hazard and segment-predictive likelihood. Adams–MacKay and Fearnhead–Liu give online recursions. [E4](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Need likelihoods that remain valid inside a segment. Exact run-length bank grows with the stream, bounded truncation adds approximation. Attractive for uncertainty over onset, less direct for recall without a recurring-state model. |
| Hidden Markov model | Sum filtered state weights through a transition matrix then multiply by emissions. Small finite HMM costs O(K²) per look for dense transitions. [E4](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Recurrence and dwell structure can pool evidence. Emission similarity and duration mismatch can lock into an old state. Forward filtering only. A bounded HMM is the challenger if simple thresholds react too slowly at matched false alarms. |
| Online Bayesian filtering | Propagate parameter uncertainty, update on incoming observations. Exact on a small grid with tractable emissions; particle filters approximate nonlinear/high-dimensional state updates. Surface-code tensor-network likelihood with sequential Monte Carlo illustrates benefit and identifiability limits under its own model. [E4, E6](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Grid easy for small nuisance dimensions, particle filtering/TN expensive and potentially unstable in short segments. A richer model needs joint-emission validation and latency accounting. |
| Regime-switching state-space model | Combine Markov regime with continuous latent drift dynamics. Kim's filtering estimates latent switching and within-regime state. [E4](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | High tuning and model complexity. May help separate drifting background and abrupt support changes with enough development coverage. Filter forward and freeze dynamics before evaluating. |

## C. Faster estimation

| Method | Principle and evidence | Assumption, difficulty and Q-SHIFT role |
|---|---|---|
| Short adaptive windows | Reset or shorten history after a signal. ADWIN compares subwindow means with bounded-observation assumptions. [E5](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Light update for few summaries, substantial revalidation for E1T and the flip stage. False flags in the existing AD3 screen prevent treating one to three blocks as interchangeable with six. [Q7](REFERENCES.md#q-shift-evidence) |
| Overlapping windows | Re-estimate at intervals shorter than window duration to improve update granularity. Bhardwaj et al. quantify trailing-window lag/variance for smooth drift. [E5](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Repeated estimates share data. A small stride leaves within-window old-regime bias. Cache sums and calibrate repeated-look thresholds. A strong control for gains from observation cadence alone. |
| Multiple timescales | Short and long summaries separate change evidence from stable risk/background fitting. Combine or choose based on a predeclared rule. [E5](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Medium effort. Correlated windows and search over scales inflate the maximum under the null. Good architecture if the small history prefix sees a burst while the background estimate is still adequate. |
| EWMA | \(m_t=(1-\rho)m_{t-1}+\rho x_t\) trades smoothness for phase lag. Roberts shows geometric moving averages as control charts. [E5](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Minimal memory/cost. Large gain transfers measurement noise to switches; small gain smears abrupt changes. Tune on development at matched alarm budget and retain as a strong baseline. |
| Streaming detector-feature updates | Update marginal, selected parity and/or cofire counts per fresh row. Spitz et al. learn matching weights from syndrome correlations; Chen et al. characterize noise using observed syndrome correlations on hardware. [E5, E9](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Counts are cheap with a small predetermined feature set. Joint law and covariance still matter; changing features changes identifiability. An efficient data path for all arms, useful for separately testing prefix availability. |

## D. Decoder selection

| Strategy | Mechanism and evidence | Implementation and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Precomputed bank | Build immutable decoders for a bounded set, then activate a key. LILLIPUT illustrates precomputation versus memory in a different decoder. Q-SHIFT already caches or recalls by keys. [E9](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) · [Q5, Q6](REFERENCES.md#q-shift-evidence) | Medium cost for BP+OSD memory and key coverage. Measure activation separately from construction and decode. It reduces computation after evidence arrives. |
| Immediate switch with hysteresis | Execute at the first eligible batch after evidence and asymmetric entry/exit thresholds. Optional dwell prevents churn. Related to sequential stopping under distinct boundaries. [E2](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Low engineering cost. A fixed dwell delays real OFF/return events, so impose the smallest dwell justified by development operating points. |
| Confidence-aware switch | Require evidence for the winner against close alternatives and a reject region, as in decision theory with rejection. [E9](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Moderate calibration effort. Non-rejection of a profile is insufficient. Rejecting a model still produces a prediction and contributes to unconditional LER. |
| Risk-sensitive fallback | Hold the last accepted decoder where validated, otherwise use a pooled robust static key. Gate using estimated logical loss differences and uncertainty. [E9](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) · [Q3, Q4](REFERENCES.md#q-shift-evidence) | Medium effort to train a cross-state loss table. A stale decoder can be costly on new ON segments, and a pooled model can be inferior in a stable state. A direct comparison tests both. |
| Mixture of experts | Average calibrated \(P(L\mid D,s)\) with state weights \(P(s\mid\text{past},D)\) if modelling coherent joint prediction, or freeze weights based on available gate inputs for an empirical mixture. [E9](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | High effort. Hard BP+OSD bits supply neither posterior calibration nor probabilities for a logical-risk mixture. Running multiple decoding experts has a resource cost. |
| Soft versus hard selection | A soft belief can modulate decisions while a decoder key remains fixed; full soft decoding requires a valid likelihood/mixture representation. Hard keys make chronology and costs easy to audit. [E3, E9](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Prefer hard actuation with uncertainty tracking first. Averaging graph weights or hard parity votes generally differs from posterior averaging and requires separate justification. |

## E. What would count as prediction?

| Approach | Evidence and limitation | Q-SHIFT fit |
|---|---|---|
| Predictive noise-state tracking | Kalman extrapolation and HMM predictive distributions use previous data and a dynamics model. Huo and Li forecast simulated noise from past syndrome-derived indicators under matched temporal assumptions. [E4, E10](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Low to medium for grid filtering. Benefit plausible for persistent smooth drift, or recurring state with reliable duration. Compare against persistence. |
| Change-hazard models | Learn hazard from prior episodes or use a duration model to predict residual run length. Constant hazard gives a geometric residual independent of elapsed dwell. [E10](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Medium complexity and high risk of schedule shortcuts. Hold out change times and dwell patterns. A clock trained on the deterministic S2 schedule is not evidence of observable predictive signal. |
| Short-horizon forecasting | Multi-timescale or GP history supports future conditional means if the process has temporal correlation. [E5, E10](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | Valuable only if there is a signal before onset and gain after uncertainty/compute costs. A trailing filter observing the first changed prefix is reaction. |
| Learning-based transition prediction | Supervise alarms on future transition labels during training and evaluate strict prefix features on independent episodes. AlphaQubit shows learned logical decoding, a different target from predicting noise transitions. [E11](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) | High data and validation cost. Delay before random abrupt changes needs an available precursor, intervention or known hazard. Do not use schedule IDs/clocks, smoothed future labels as features, or overlapping fit/eval roots. |

## F. Limits shared by all methods

- **False alarms versus delay.** An earlier threshold crossing at relaxed calibration says little. Match false alarm definitions and compare wrong-switch rates and misses, with separate episode-level uncertainty. First-alarm ARL can hide a high near-term false alarm probability. [E1–E3, E8](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature)
- **Distinguishability and identifiability.** Detector laws may overlap even when decoder risks differ. Compressed statistics can further erase differences. Model-specific structural identifiability does not give short-prefix classification accuracy. See the [information and row bounds](MATHEMATICAL_FEASIBILITY.md). [E6, E7](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature)
- **Short bursts and granularity.** Recognize before the event ends and leave time to score later rows. Vary duration and offset relative to permitted looks. A response within one logged index may still miss the entire event at whole-block cadence. [E6](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature) · [Q5, Q6](REFERENCES.md#q-shift-evidence)
- **Two clocks.** Count observations to evidence and time from evidence to committed model. Faster matching or decoder hardware changes execution time. It cannot create an earlier informative observation. [E9, E11](REFERENCES.md#primary-literature)

The distinctive publishable hypothesis is that separating detection, classification and risk-aware switching recovers paired logical loss in a specified transient regime. None of the algorithm components is claimed as new. Direct tests of first occurrences, repeated returns and predecision information are required for a broader claim.
