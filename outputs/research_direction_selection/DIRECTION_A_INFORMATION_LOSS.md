# Direction A — Information loss in circuit-to-DEM decoding

## Question

How much do detector-error-model (DEM) conversion and graphlike decomposition change the distribution presented to a matching decoder, and when do those changes alter its logical-observable decision under categorical or correlated circuit faults?

## Established science and software behavior

Stim’s DEM format describes a list of independent error mechanisms, each associated with detector symptoms and logical-observable flips. A categorical `PAULI_CHANNEL_1` or `PAULI_CHANNEL_2` instead selects mutually exclusive Pauli outcomes. Stim documents that converting such a channel may require `approximate_disjoint_errors=True`; for example, a channel with disjoint X and Y outcomes can be approximated by independent X and Y mechanisms. `decompose_errors=True` further suggests graphlike components for faults with more than two detector symptoms. These are explicit modeling operations, not evidence of a decoder bug. See [Stim’s conversion documentation](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim/blob/main/doc/usage_command_line.md) and [DEM semantics](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim/blob/main/doc/file_format_dem_detector_error_model.md).

Stim samples the original circuit-level channel directly, whereas PyMatching consumes the derived graph. The codebase’s Phase 2B audit correctly records this distinction. Phase 2C’s sample-trained random forest used detector/observable samples from the actual categorical channel but performed substantially worse than matching (2,579 vs 628 errors on 120,000 pilot shots). Since the reference itself was weak and approximate, this does **not** quantify the causal effect of DEM conversion or decomposition.

## Research value

This direction addresses an important representation question: whether observable error-pattern probabilities discarded or approximated in a graph model matter for logical decisions. A useful initial study could compare direct-circuit samples with samples generated by the approximate DEM, measuring detector/observable marginal and selected joint-pattern differences, then assess each decoder on direct-circuit shots. That would quantify distribution mismatch and practical decoding consequences separately.

## Feasibility and risk

- **Reuse:** High for Stim circuits, raw paired predictions, DEM files, exact toy enumeration and integrity tooling.
- **Implementation:** Medium-to-high for credible utility attribution; high if a true categorical-channel posterior decoder is required.
- **Compute:** Circuit-vs-DEM Monte Carlo comparison is moderate. Exact full-circuit posterior enumeration is infeasible at current circuit size; Phase 2C’s naïve upper count was `2^609` fault assignments for its d=3, five-round model.
- **Measurability:** Distributional mismatch is measurable. Whether that mismatch materially harms decoder LER is harder to isolate without a validated alternative decoder.
- **Competition value:** High conceptual value if explained as “what the decoder’s simplified map forgets,” but challenging to explain and easy to overclaim.
- **Risk:** High risk of another inconclusive result. A distribution difference need not change a matching correction, and a worse learned model cannot diagnose the approximation.

## Literature and novelty boundary

Stim’s paper explains its fast stabilizer and Pauli-frame simulation, not a general guarantee that a DEM approximation preserves decoder utility ([Gidney, *Quantum* 5, 497 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2021-07-06-497)). Stim’s own documentation states which conversions are approximate. Circuit-level likelihood and near-maximum-likelihood decoding are established topics; the open part here would be a controlled, validated attribution of the practical effect of this specific representation choice for the project’s specified channel families. No novelty claim is made until relevant work and a stronger reference decoder are assessed more fully.

## Assessment

Scientifically meaningful, but not the best first competition pilot. Preserve it as a follow-up if the simpler correlation-mode ablation shows that the available DEM correlation information matters. Do not call Phase 2C proof that DEM approximation limits performance.
