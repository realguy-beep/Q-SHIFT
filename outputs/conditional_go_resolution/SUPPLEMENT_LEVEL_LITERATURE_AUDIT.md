# Supplement-level literature audit

**Reviewed:** 6 November 2026. Scope was the closest primary work on syndrome-history noise estimation, time-varying matching weights, and decoder-prior updates. This is a focused prior-art audit, not a systematic review of every decoder paper.

## Sources and methods examined

| Work | Sections and supplement material inspected | Findings relevant to Q-SHIFT |
|---|---|---|
| Spitz, Tarasinski, Beenakker & O’Brien (2018), [“Adaptive Weight Estimator for Quantum Error Correction in a Time-Dependent Environment”](https://doi.org/10.1002/qute.201800012), *Advanced Quantum Technologies* 1, 1800012 | Publisher full text: Sec. 3.2–3.4 (pair/boundary edge probabilities and complexity), Algorithm 1, Sec. 4.1–4.2, Sec. 5, and Appendices A–B. No separate supplementary file was exposed on the publisher record reviewed. | Direct precedent for estimating matching-edge probabilities from syndrome correlations without a known physical noise model, updating estimates from prior rounds, and recalculating MWPM weights in a time-dependent environment. The demonstration is a bit-flip repetition-code model at distances 3, 5 and 7; window length trades estimation variance against lag. The authors explicitly state adaptation fails when noise changes faster than sufficient estimation data can be acquired. This is not a rotated surface-code circuit-level benchmark. |
| Huo & Li (2017), [“Learning time-dependent noise to reduce logical errors: Real time error rate estimation in quantum error correction”](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/19/12/123032), *New Journal of Physics* 19, 123032 | Full preprint text: Sec. III (syndrome-pattern and correction-operation event estimators), Sec. IV (online Gaussian-process update), Sec. V (simulation), Sec. VI and Appendices A–C. | Explicitly causal: the paper states that the error-rate estimate from round *t* is not used for that round, and only affects round *t+1*. It estimates rates from past QEC data and simulates time-varying dephasing with an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck prior. It is a conceptual and technical precedent for causal syndrome-only estimation and next-round decoder-weight updates. The numerical model is independent dephasing, and observed error-event indicators are acknowledged to be approximate. |
| Bhardwaj, Takou, Lin & Brown (2026), [“Adaptive Estimation of Drifting Noise in Quantum Error Correction”](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1hc-nqw5), *PRX Quantum* 7, 033024 | Full 23-page APS article PDF, including Sec. II–V and Appendices A–C. The APS record exposes the paper and appendices; no separate supplemental PDF was linked there. The article also links a public implementation. | Direct precedent for syndrome-history-only estimation and reconstruction of time-varying DEMs. Sec. II–III develop sliding, iterative and relative/overlapping-window estimates; Table I quantifies drift-period resolution (approximately 1000 cycles for conventional/iterative windows and 200 cycles for the relative-window method). Sec. IV.E compares estimated, ground-truth and static DEM decoding on common detection-event samples; Figs. 9–11 cover repetition and rotated-surface-code simulations, including circuit-level examples. Appendices A–C provide estimator-variance and noise derivations. |
| Sivak et al. (2026), [“Reinforcement learning control of quantum error correction”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10759-2), *Nature* 655, 879–884; [Supplementary Information record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13391382/) | Published main article sections “Learning from errors,” “RL steering of control policy,” and “Learning algorithm” were reviewed. The final SI PDF is listed on the open-access record but its file endpoint was not retrievable in this tool session. The accessible arXiv v1 SI dated 8 Dec 2025 was reviewed for Sec. I.B, II, III, V.A and VI; these are preprint-SI page references, not verified final-SI page references. | The main result adapts more than decoder weights: a reinforcement-learning agent continuously changes physical control parameters using detector statistics. It evaluates step-like, sinusoidal and stroboscopic injected drift and reports a measured response time for control steering. The accessible preprint SI’s Sec. VI says the decoder prior was retrained between evaluation epochs using data from previous epochs, decoupled from control-parameter training and performed after the steering experiment from stored data. This is temporally causal, but it is not a live per-block decoder-update loop. The final published SI could add or revise details; this remains an evidence-access gap. |
| Wang et al. (2023), [DGR: “Tackling Drifted and Correlated Noise in Quantum Error Correction via Decoding Graph Re-weighting”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16214), arXiv:2311.16214 | Full arXiv manuscript: Sec. III–V, especially Sec. IV.A–B and V.B. This is a preprint, not treated here as peer-reviewed evidence. | Direct algorithmic precedent for using history from prior MWPM matchings to estimate edge frequencies, reweight later decoding, and optionally do a second correlation-aware matching pass. The paper describes periodic or drift-triggered alignment updates and reports a small reweighter latency range. Its estimator uses decoder outputs derived from syndrome data, rather than only raw detector marginals. |
| Chen et al. (2022), [“Calibrated Decoders for Experimental Quantum Error Correction”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.110504), *Physical Review Letters* 128, 110504 | Full APS manuscript: theory of correlation analysis and decoder calibration sections. | Demonstrates experimentally calibrated matching weights that account for correlated syndrome events on a heavy-hexagon QEC experiment. This is relevant static-calibration prior art and illustrates that matching weights can be learned from QEC measurements. It is not a study of online drift tracking. |
| Sivak, Newman & Klimov (2024), [“Optimization of Decoder Priors for Accurate Quantum Error Correction”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.150603), *Physical Review Letters* 133, 150603 | APS abstract and accessible publication metadata; full text was not accessible from APS without credentials in this review. | Reinforcement-learning-inspired calibration of decoder priors for repetition and surface-code memories on Sycamore. It is relevant to decoder-prior optimization, but the full methods/supplement could not be independently inspected here; no claim is made about its online drift or resource controls. |
| Remm et al. (2026), [“Experimentally informed decoding of stabilizer codes based on syndrome correlations”](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1ng-wg3k), *Physical Review Research* 8, 013044 | APS article abstract and accessible article text around the method description. | Experimental syndrome-correlation calibration of matching weights on a distance-three surface code, including Pauli-Y, hook, multi-qubit and leakage-related events. It reinforces the state of the art in decoder calibration; its described contribution is model calibration rather than the proposed Q-SHIFT online update controller. |

## Limits of the audit

- The Bhardwaj paper’s appendices were available and inspected; a separate supplement was not linked from the APS record reviewed.
- The published Nature main article and metadata for its final SI were available, but the final SI PDF endpoint could not be retrieved in this tool session. The accessible Dec. 2025 arXiv v1 supplement was inspected instead and is explicitly distinguished above. Custom code is described as proprietary in the published article. The final-SI text remains an unresolved coverage gap for claims that depend on supplemental details.
- The full text of the 2024 PRL decoder-prior paper was paywalled in the APS route used here. It is included as relevant prior art, but its supplementary methods are not independently verified in this audit.
- Where a table below says a control or statistic was “not identified,” that means it was not found in the reviewed methods/material—not proof that the authors never performed it.
