## Answers to the six novelty checks

1. **Causal syndrome-only updates:** already evaluated in principle by Huo–Li and Spitz; Bhardwaj provides a recent syndrome-history DEM estimator and decoder evaluation. DGR adds matching-history weight updates. The core mechanism is not new.
2. **Abrupt and gradual drift:** step-like and gradual/sinusoidal drift are already represented in the literature. Exact Q-SHIFT schedule variants are not a novelty by themselves.
3. **Strong pooled-fixed baseline:** the reviewed works often use a static nominal model or an oracle/ground-truth model. A calibration-selected strongest pooled baseline is a worthwhile control, but it does not make the adaptation mechanism novel.
4. **Equally resourced periodic recalibration:** not identified as a fully resource-matched comparator in the nearest works reviewed. This is a real experimental-design gap, not evidence that online adaptation is novel or beneficial.
5. **Latency, compute, tracking delay:** prior work reports some analytic complexity, windows, or selected latencies, but not all end-to-end costs under a shared schedule and resource budget. Q-SHIFT’s local rebuild measurement is far below a full hardware feasibility case.
6. **Paired logical-error significance:** Bhardwaj uses common detection samples for model comparisons, but the nearest literature reviewed does not supply the exact root-cluster paired inferential protocol proposed here. That is a benchmark-quality opportunity, not a sufficient new scientific hypothesis on its own.

## Decision implication

The literature leaves room to improve **comparison quality**: pooled-fixed strength, equal-data/equal-compute periodic controls, whole-schedule false-update control, and paired uncertainty. It does not leave a defensible claim that Q-SHIFT is introducing causal syndrome-only matching-weight adaptation. A paper on those controls would need a distinctly framed benchmark contribution and a literature search beyond this focused set; it should not be presented as a new adaptation algorithm.
