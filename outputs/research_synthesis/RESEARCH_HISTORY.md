# Q-SHIFT research history and evidence reconstruction

## Scope and overall conclusion

This synthesis reviews saved protocols, reports, structured results, source descriptions, and audits for the original prototype, Phase 1, Phases 2A–2C, and later Phase 3–5 work relevant to publication readiness. It does not resample, rerun decoders, regenerate manifests, or change historical files. “Verified” refers to repository audits or independent prediction rescoring, not external validity.

The current working directory is not a Git checkout, so commit history and Git timestamps were not available; chronology is reconstructed from saved protocol/freeze records, logs, manifests and audit reports.

Q-SHIFT became a functioning, reproducible simulation and evaluation framework, with valuable negative and conditional findings. The saved evidence does not establish a novel general adaptive-decoding method or reliable adaptive improvement. A publication needs a narrower question and new independent experiments.

## Starter and Phase 1: recognition is not utility

The starter used Stim’s distance-3 repetition-code memory circuit with three measurement rounds, eight detectors and one logical observable. Its detector vector admits only 256 patterns. The 150,000-row dataset contained 50,000 samples at each of three noise settings, but only 61 unique detector vectors and 117 unique complete rows; 149,883 rows repeated. It had no sample identifiers or provenance manifest. Row-random splitting could put repeated examples in both partitions, so early classification scores are weak evidence of generalization. Early conversation metrics not reproducible from current artifacts are excluded.

Phase 1 held a distance-3, three-round repetition-memory circuit structure fixed while testing X-dominant, Z-dominant, depolarizing, measurement-only, mixed X/Z and data-plus-measurement conditions. Noise was inserted after CNOT participants using single-qubit Pauli channels; measurement flips were inserted before Z-basis measurement locations. Equal local probabilities did not mean equal total circuit fault opportunities. Across ten seed families, detector windows recognized trained regimes well (94.4% in the recorded time-varying stream), but validation rejected all guarded lookup switches. The guarded policy made no switches and tied fixed PyMatching; an earlier rate-only selector switched and was worse. Thus regime recognition alone is insufficient when candidate decoder utility is not established. Noise was stationary within each circuit shot and changed between batches, not within a circuit.

## Phase 2: specialist models and decoder fidelity

**Phase 2 specialist investigation** retained the repetition-code circuit and compared nominal PyMatching with specialists for X-, Z-, depolarizing and measurement-only noise. X-, Z- and depolarizing-specialist weights changed but their logical predictions matched nominal on all tested samples. Measurement-only matching changed few predictions and was generally worse. The validation gate selected fixed matching in every condition and seed (60/60 choices), so no selector was built. This is a result for the tested graph and noise range, not a theorem about matching.

**Phase 2A** moved to a distance-3, five-round rotated surface-code Z-memory circuit with 40 detectors, one observable and a nine-point gate/measurement probability grid. Calibration selected zero-based candidate 1 globally and for every generating regime. On 600,000 held-out shots, global and per-regime calibration both made 4,192 errors; their paired difference and interval were zero, and root sign-flip p=1 because they made identical predictions. Retrospective best-on-test candidates did not pass the multiplicity gate. Some models had identical observed logical decisions; that is sample-specific, not proof of decoder equivalence.

**Phase 2B** varied X/Y/Z composition at fixed total local gate-fault probability in distance-3, five-round rotated surface-code X- and Z-memory. Six basis-by-composition cells had 20 roots and 10,000 paired shots/root (1.2 million total). Calibration selected one model per memory basis, unchanged across generating compositions: model 0 for X-memory and model 2 for Z-memory. Regime-informed and global choices tied at 5,532/1,200,000 errors, 95% interval [0,0], p=1. The balanced fixed model made 6,644 errors. The defensible result is basis-specific fixed-model selection in these simulated conditions, not composition-specific adaptation. The sampled channel was categorical Pauli noise, but the matching DEM used Stim’s approximate disjoint-error conversion.

**Phase 2C** tested whether DEM approximation might limit specialization using random forests trained from labeled circuit samples as posterior surrogates. These were not exact full-circuit likelihood decoders. On 120,000 pilot shots the selected surrogate made 2,579 errors (2.1492%), versus 628 (0.5233%) for Phase 2B regime-matched PyMatching. Surrogates disagreed with matching on some predictions, but were much worse and calibration again selected the same candidate across noise regimes within each basis. This neither proves nor disproves that DEM approximation limits matching: the surrogate was too weak to isolate that causal factor. Four roots/cell makes this a feasibility pilot.

## Later follow-up: replication, baseline strength, and prototype

Phase 3A initially used an invalid channel-probability convention. Corrected versions reused the same three raw seed bundles and are not independent repetitions. A small corrected X-bias pilot appeared eight errors better in 36,000 shots, but did not survive multiplicity correction. Phase 3B used 20 fresh roots and 600,000 primary X-biased shots: fixed made 43,673 errors and the specialist 43,675; paired p=.9374 and the seed-bootstrap interval crossed zero. The pilot did not replicate.

Phase 4A used native single- and two-qubit depolarizing errors plus measurement noise in distance-3, five-round rotated Z-memory. Under measurement-heavy noise (gate p=.001, measurement p=.006), calibrated PyMatching made 940 errors versus 1,118 for nominal fixed across 600,000 paired shots. Candidate-only/fixed-only discordances were 80/258; exact McNemar p=5.600789e-23; seed-bootstrap 95% interval for candidate-minus-fixed rate about [-.0003667, -.0002217], with 19/20 roots favoring calibration. Data-heavy calibration was slightly worse; nominal tied. Independent integrity reaudit passed scientific artifacts after explaining three Markdown hash mismatches. This supports static calibration against a nominal baseline, not adaptation.

Phase 4B used new primary test roots under the same measurement-heavy condition. Independent re-decoding found fixed 1,165 vs calibrated 1,008 errors/600,000; candidate-only/fixed-only failures were 99/256; exact McNemar p=3.730723e-17; 20/20 roots favored calibration. The audit was **PASS WITH DEVIATION** because the executed runner generated a fresh template bank although protocol text specified reuse. This is a real method deviation; the result replicates a static comparison against nominal, not adaptive selection.

Phase 4C Stage 1 used 1.2 million development shots to choose stronger fixed baselines. Stage 2 compared calibrated decoding with pooled, nominal, minimax and condition-specific development selections on 1.8 million paired held-out shots across data-heavy, nominal and measurement-heavy settings. Equal-regime calibrated-minus-pooled was +0.00001556; seed-stratified 95% CI [-0.00004889, +0.00007667], sign-flip p=.66658. No improvement over pooled fixed was found. All 60 test prediction bundles were independently re-decoded, but calibration arrays and scoring traces were not saved, so fitting cannot be independently reconstructed. RNG allocation required prospective amendments; this weakens an unqualified preregistration claim. Treat it as a strong held-out, independently rescored null result with incomplete calibration provenance.

Phase 5 repaired early adaptive-evaluation mapping and retained corrected, versioned results. In the audited prototype-development dataset (50 roots, 307,200 paired shots), identity/no-correction made 19,726 errors (6.4212%), pooled fixed 1,989 (0.6475%), nominal fixed 1,976 (0.6432%) and adaptive 1,989 (0.6475%). Adaptive tied pooled because it retained the fallback. Identity means “predict no logical flip”; it is not a physical circuit without QEC. Phase 5 is development-only, with no fresh independent adaptive test.

## Current scientific status

The strongest current conclusion is conditional: calibrated weights beat an intentionally nominal decoder in a particular measurement-heavy condition in Phase 4A and Phase 4B; a stronger pooled fixed baseline removes that advantage in the Phase 4C comparison. The prototype has causal detector estimation and fallback behavior, but no reliable adaptive advantage. The research question should be when calibration creates usable decoder headroom and whether causal detector data can exploit it, not whether Q-SHIFT already wins.
