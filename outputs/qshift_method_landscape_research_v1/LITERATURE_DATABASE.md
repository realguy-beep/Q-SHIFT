### Wang et al., DGR
- **Citation/source:** Hanrui Wang et al., “DGR: Tackling Drifted and Correlated Noise in Quantum Error Correction via Decoding Graph Re-weighting,” arXiv:2311.16214v3 (22 Apr 2024), https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16214.
- **Method/model information:** Counts graph edges and edge pairs in previous MWPM matchings; alignment stage uses empirical matching frequencies to reweight edges; correlation stage applies heuristic or learned pair adjustments and re-decodes difficult cases. The article describes this as operation on preceding decoding trials, either periodically or upon drift.
- **Evidence/baseline:** Paper reports surface-code and honeycomb-code simulations under its specified settings and large mismatch stress; it compares to its decoding baselines. Claims belong to that paper’s simulation distributions and metrics.
- **Limitations relevant to Q-SHIFT:** Matching events are inferred pseudo-labels, so estimates depend on base decoder correctness. It estimates graph-edge statistics, which need not identify Q-SHIFT's latent common-cause burst probability. Paper itself indicates sample demand depends on physical rate and illustrates convergence near 10^6 trials in one distance-5, p=0.001 example. It includes NN-trained reweighting in addition to its heuristic; that entails supervised loss/model training not proposed for the current no-training path. No Q-SHIFT transfer established.
- **Evidence reviewed:** abstract, alignment and correlation methodology, selected evaluation passages. No upstream implementation audit yet.

### Bhardwaj et al., drift estimation
- **Citation/source:** Devansh Bhardwaj, Evangelia Takou, Yingjia Lin, Kenneth R. Brown, “Adaptive Estimation of Drifting Noise in Quantum Error Correction,” *PRX Quantum* 7, 033024 (2026), DOI `10.1103/z1hc-nqw5`; [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1hc-nqw5), [arXiv:2511.09491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09491).
- **Method/model information:** Uses syndrome statistics and edge-based decoding graphs for time-dependent Pauli-noise rates; analyzes sliding-window noise filtering and overlapping/relative-window rate estimates. Its inversion formulas assume independent graph-event mechanisms and recover effective graph rates. Q-SHIFT's injected X8/X10 event is itself Bernoulli and independent across the five round boundaries, while its two physical X flips are spatially common-cause. If each round-specific event maps to a distinct graphlike detector pair and other contributions satisfy the inversion assumptions, the method may contain a route to estimating the burst rate; this mapping has not been derived here.
- **Evidence/baseline:** Peer-reviewed paper reports phenomenological and circuit-level simulations in its code/noise settings, including decoder comparisons with estimated models. One described estimation study uses 50,000 syndrome-extraction cycles and windows around 2,000 cycles; the authors note the temporal index can instead correspond to shots. This indicates a substantial history/data-volume scale in that example, not a transferable Q-SHIFT window prescription because each Q-SHIFT shot has a different detector structure and its sample covariance is unmeasured. These are paper results, not Q-SHIFT evidence.
- **Limitations relevant to Q-SHIFT:** A deterministic Stim 1.16.0 structural audit under the declared p_gate=p_meas=0.003 template found each of the five injected burst detector supports absent from the baseline approximate DEM and present once at p=0.01 in the burst-inclusive DEM (586→591 mechanisms). This removes an exact-support collision in that DEM representation and makes graph-moment estimation more plausible there. It does not establish that the exact circuit distribution equals the DEM, that all categorical gate alternatives satisfy independent-edge assumptions, that the 45-feature population map identifies burst p separately from measurement/gate nuisance, or that finite histories are adequate. The full five-round boundary structure must still be respected. “Syndrome-only” does not by itself establish that the estimated graph minimizes Q-SHIFT logical risk. Do not transfer reported LER gains.
- **Evidence reviewed:** publisher metadata/abstract, arXiv full text and selected equations/method passages. Linked public notebook repository is marked under construction, has no inspected release pin/license, and was not run or fully audited.

### Remm et al., syndrome-correlation calibration
- **Citation/source:** Ants Remm et al., “Experimentally informed decoding of stabilizer codes based on syndrome correlations,” *Physical Review Research* 8, 013044 (2026), DOI `10.1103/z1ng-wg3k`; [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1ng-wg3k), [arXiv:2502.17722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17722).
- **Method/model information:** Uses analytical higher-order syndrome correlations to estimate probabilities of independent error events with unique syndrome signatures, aiming to calibrate a circuit-matched model without a separate calibration experiment.
- **Evidence/baseline:** Publisher abstract and full-text assumption audit confirm an experimental distance-three surface-code setting and model calibration/decoder-weight use for that system.
- **Limitations relevant to Q-SHIFT:** Unique-signature assumptions and event independence matter. A latent joint burst whose signature aliases other mechanisms may not be estimable from the same statistics. Static calibration is not online causal drift validation.
- **Evidence reviewed:** publisher metadata/abstract, full arXiv text and selected derivation/assumption sections. This is not a full proof/code replication.

### Miao et al., QAdapt (v2, latest checked 10 Nov 2026)
- **Citation/source:** Ran Miao et al., “QAdapt: Noise-Adaptive Neural Pre-Decoding for Surface-Code Quantum Error Correction,” arXiv:2607.28422v2 (revised 10 Nov 2026), [v2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28422v2); the arXiv history lists v1 (30 Jul) and v2 (10 Nov).
- **Method/model information:** A 650,332-parameter HTNet predicts a local correction from a four-channel space-time syndrome tensor; PyMatching then decodes the residual using the same graph/prior within each comparison condition. Sequential task adaptation uses Online EWC. Training uses simulated correction targets and a 25-parameter circuit noise model; the controlled sequence changes measurement, idle, CNOT and Z-bias regimes. It is supervised continual neural pre-decoding, not a latent burst-rate estimator or a graph-switch controller.
- **Evidence/baseline:** v2 reports a paired d=9 architecture comparison over 1,048,576 shots per basis, with equivalent per-round LER 15.64% lower than Ising-Fast (95% interval 15.10–16.18%) and matching-backend time 9.07% lower. On a fixed 331-condition valid subset of unseen compound-noise settings, frozen Online EWC is 5.66% better than T0-only; on native Willow records it reports 17.47–19.44% lower equivalent per-round rates at 10/30/50 rounds without target-domain fine-tuning. All methods share records within case, and the matching prior is held the same within each evaluation condition.
- **Limitations relevant to Q-SHIFT:** v2 states each training configuration has one realized training seed, so evaluation intervals are conditional on trained weights. It requires simulated labeled correction targets and a sequentially adapted neural stack; its noise axes, d=9 surface-code setup and residual-predecoder objective differ from Q-SHIFT's d=3 five-round known-support pair-burst graph switching. It is important if the owner permits supervised pretraining, not a fair same-information detector-only baseline by default. Preprint results are not independently reproduced here; local implementation/API and released weights were not audited.
- **Evidence reviewed:** arXiv v2 metadata, full HTML framework, simulated training/evaluation design, selected results, paired uncertainty and appendices. No model, code, training, or evaluation was run.

### Change-detection foundations
- **Page CUSUM:** E. S. Page, “Continuous Inspection Schemes,” Biometrika 41 (1954), 100–115, DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100. Foundational sequential likelihood-ratio accumulation; Q-SHIFT use would require a predeclared detector-feature null/alternative, calibration of false alarms under correlated shots/features, and reset/dwell policy.
- **ADWIN:** Albert Bifet and Ricard Gavaldà, “Learning from Time-Changing Data with Adaptive Windowing,” original 2006 paper (PDF: https://www.cs.upc.edu/~Gavalda/papers/adwin06.pdf). Adaptive window length with concentration-bound guarantees under stated data assumptions. Its guarantees cannot simply be transferred to temporally dependent multi-feature detector records or a decoder-selection objective.
- **BOCPD:** Ryan P. Adams and David J. C. MacKay, “Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection,” 2007 (author PDF: https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~rpa/pubs/adams2007changepoint.pdf). Online posterior over run length assumes within-segment i.i.d. data and a specified predictive likelihood/hazard model; a multivariate Bernoulli/history model and computation bound would be necessary here.

### Bayesian syndrome-statistics inference (published 2025)
- **Citation/source:** Takumi Kobori and Synge Todo, “Bayesian inference of general noise-model parameters from the syndrome statistics of surface codes,” *Physical Review A* 112, 052448 (24 Nov 2025), DOI [10.1103/wg5h-spy6](https://doi.org/10.1103/wg5h-spy6); [arXiv:2406.08981](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08981).
- **Method/model information:** Likelihood over measured syndrome outcomes is computed using a tensor-network surface-code simulator. Stationary parameters use MCMC; evolving parameters use sequential Monte Carlo particles with transition proposals, periodic resampling and optional smoothing. This is a generative-likelihood alternative to fitting low-order empirical detector features.
- **Evidence/baseline:** The paper studies static/time-varying/nonuniform amplitude damping and related cases with code-capacity and other model settings, including d=3/5/7 examples. It reports parameter recovery and decoder performance, with sampling-based likelihood/posterior work.
- **Limitations relevant to Q-SHIFT:** Requires the correct generative circuit/noise family and expensive likelihood evaluation; MCMC/SMC adds Monte Carlo uncertainty and state. The paper itself reports a parameter that was difficult to estimate where the likelihood was weakly sensitive to it, illustrating identifiability is model/parameter specific. It is not a ready-made solution to a categorical correlated burst, nor can its stochastic inference be reproduced under this task's no-sampling/no-stochastic-simulation constraint.
- **Evidence reviewed:** publisher record and full arXiv HTML methods/selected results; no source code audit.

### Algebraic adaptive matching weights
- **Citation/source:** S. T. Spitz, B. Tarasinski, C. W. J. Beenakker and T. E. O’Brien, “Adaptive weight estimator for quantum error correction in a time-dependent environment,” *Advanced Quantum Technologies* (2018), article 1800012, DOI [10.1002/qute.201800012](https://doi.org/10.1002/qute.201800012); [arXiv:1712.02360](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.02360).
- **Method/model information:** Infers matching edge weights algebraically from syndrome covariance for pair-connected vertices and separately estimates boundary-edge weights; updates based on measured data without a calibrated physical noise model. The article analyzes a repetition-code construction and time-dependent Pauli setting.
- **Evidence/baseline:** Authors report useful adaptation if environment time scale exceeds approximately cycle duration divided by typical per-qubit error probability; theoretical/simulation assumptions are theirs.
- **Limitations relevant to Q-SHIFT:** Edge inversion relies on the decoding-graph independent-event structure. The slow-drift condition can imply long evidence requirements at small physical rates. Q-SHIFT's finite d=3 five-round shot is not the same temporal sample unit as an ongoing repeated-round code stream; its categorical joint-burst probability is not identified automatically by independent-edge estimates.
- **Evidence reviewed:** article abstract and full arXiv HTML introduction / outline; full derivation extraction pending.

### Gaussian-process adaptation
- **Citation/source:** Ming-Xia Huo and Ying Li, “Learning time-dependent noise to reduce logical errors: Real time error rate estimation in quantum error correction,” *New Journal of Physics* 19, 123032 (2017), DOI [10.1088/1367-2630/aa916e](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/aa916e); [arXiv:1710.03636](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03636).
- **Method/model information:** Uses correction-operation and syndrome-pattern approximations to estimate rates, then online Gaussian processes to predict future rates from past QEC data.
- **Evidence/baseline:** The abstract/full text describes numerical data generation and an ideal decoder using the estimated rates, with gains that scale with code distance under its scenarios.
- **Limitations relevant to Q-SHIFT:** Estimates are approximate (paper gives O(epsilon^2) error for syndrome-pattern method), and the comparison uses a modeled ideal decoder, not an ordinary fixed PyMatching implementation with identical graph caching. GP prediction needs an appropriate temporal kernel and history volume; not validated for abrupt burst changes or measurement-confounding in Q-SHIFT.
- **Evidence reviewed:** arXiv abstract and full-text selected introduction/method passages; no code audit.

### Correlated matching caution (recent preprint)
- **Citation/source:** César Hernando-Fuente et al., “Correlated decoding loses the surface code’s effective distance below threshold,” arXiv:2610.26785v2 (6 Nov 2026), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26785).
- **Method/model information:** Analyzes two-pass correlated matching/weighted-union-find rules that reweight one decoding subgraph conditional on a correction in the other; this is not evidence that PyMatching's `enable_correlations` flag has the same behavior.

### Identifiability theorem and adjacent work
- **Citation/source:** Thomas Wagner, Hermann Kampermann, Dagmar Bruß, Martin Kliesch, “Pauli channels can be estimated from syndrome measurements in quantum error correction,” *Quantum* 6, 809 (2022), DOI [10.22331/q-2022-09-19-809](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2022-09-19-809), [arXiv:2107.14252](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.14252).
- **Result:** Under the paper's independent local channel-support model, nonzero Fourier moments, and support/detectability conditions, syndrome statistics identify the Pauli noise distribution when correlations are confined to `floor((d_p - 1)/2)` combined qubits/measurement bits, where `d_p` is the *pure distance of the relevant stabilizer/data-syndrome code*. The measurement error channel is incorporated through the data-syndrome-code construction.
- **Relevance/limit:** This is a strong structural theorem, not a finite-window precision guarantee. It does not automatically apply to the whole Q-SHIFT circuit-level DEM or establish that an unrestricted two-qubit correlated channel is identifiable for a distance-3 model: the generic support bound and all assumptions must be instantiated for the actual data-syndrome model. Conversely it does not rule out identifying one known-support, one-parameter categorical burst family; a constrained parametric subfamily can be identifiable even when a much larger channel family is not. It also cannot validate approximate-DEM profile fitting without checking model mismatch.
- **Citation-chain:** The paper explicitly links syndrome-covariance adaptive-weight inversion (Spitz et al.) and suggests online adaptation as an application; the theorem is not itself an evaluated adaptive policy.

**Bibliographic correction:** A pre-existing local `NOISE_IDENTIFIABILITY.md` reference lists DOI `10.22331/q-2022-09-22-809`; the publisher verifies DOI `10.22331/q-2022-09-19-809`. This is logged as a citation correction for final reporting; the historical file is preserved unchanged.

### Remm et al. full assumption audit (published 2026)
Publisher metadata verifies Ants Remm et al., “Experimentally informed decoding of stabilizer codes based on syndrome correlations,” *Physical Review Research* 8, 013044 (16 Jan 2026), DOI [10.1103/z1ng-wg3k](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1ng-wg3k), also [arXiv:2502.17722](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17722).

Their method estimates probabilities for **independent error processes** from syndrome correlation moments. The paper derives formulas recursively by signature weight; for example, its two-element signature formula is recovered under the explicit assumption that there are no errors with signatures on more than two syndrome elements, and its four-element expression is shown under no signatures above four. Higher-support probabilities require correspondingly higher-order syndrome moments and assumptions/solutions for larger supports. Thus it can identify and calibrate a known factorized signature model, including multi-detector and leakage-like signatures in their distance-3 surface-code experimental data, but it does not make arbitrary correlated processes identifiable from the 45 Q-SHIFT features. A categorical joint event can be modeled as one independent latent event per boundary only when this independent-event decomposition and signature uniqueness match the actual circuit mechanism. Overlapping gate and measurement causes with the same detector signature remain a confounding issue unless additional signatures/circuit constraints separate them.

**Q-SHIFT implication:** The existing 45-feature profile deliberately selects five burst-pair cofire indicators in addition to all detector marginals. This is a model-specific compression, not the full hierarchy of moments used by the general correlation-calibration method. It may be adequate for the known one-parameter family, as prior deterministic checks suggest, but does not justify joint estimation of unrestricted noise or measurement mismatch. Adaptation requires rolling-window uncertainty and a model-adequacy test, not direct use of a formula derived under a different independent-edge model.

### Hoyt et al., decoder-model compatibility under drift/correlation
- **Citation/source:** Aaron C. Hoyt et al., “Decoder Model Compatibility Provides Information beyond the Logical Gap under Drifting and Correlated Quantum Noise,” arXiv:2609.29018 (submitted 24 Sep 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29018 and full text https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29018.
- **Primary-source passages reviewed:** abstract; definitions of sector free energy, logical gap, detector-history surprisal, MWPM complementary gap/minimum cost; controlled model-mismatch setup; Appendix C constrained stationary graphlike DEM fit and higher-order parity diagnostic.
- **Method and scope:** Adds minimum matching cost `W` as a cheap proxy for detector-history surprisal/model compatibility alongside complementary MWPM logical gap `Δ`, then ranks records for postselection. It reports hardware repetition/surface-code and cultivation results, plus controlled circuit-level burst-mixture analysis. Its controlled model draws a shot-level latent quiet/high state shared across noisy circuit locations; reference rate is preserved in expectation. For larger circuit models, reference and fitted graphlike DEMs are compared, with one/two-detector moment fits and selected higher-order parity moments used as a stationarity/model-mismatch diagnostic.
- **Relevance:** Strengthens the case that detector-history compatibility is a distinct signal from relative logical confidence and may expose model mismatch. This is relevant to designing a residual/OOD guard and diagnosing a stationary DEM fit.
- **Boundary:** Their primary decision is postselection/rejection, not causal decoder-parameter adaptation. Their analyses sometimes use held-out logical labels to evaluate conditional risk and development/validation labels to select rejection coefficients; Q-SHIFT's intended controller must not use online labels. Model compatibility does not by itself identify which physical parameter changed, prove burst-vs-measurement identifiability, or show that adapting a matching graph improves unconditional logical error rate. A detector record that is unlikely under a model may be no more logically dangerous if both logical sectors are affected similarly (the paper makes this distinction explicitly).
- **Evidence level:** Primary paper sections above reviewed; no source implementation audit or reproduction.

**Literature synthesis adjustment:** Include decoder-model compatibility/postselection as an adjacent but distinct research line. The novelty claim for “using detector mismatch to trigger adaptation” must be narrowed: mismatch scoring and its relationship to drift/correlation are already being studied. A narrower potential contribution, if later established, is a causal detector-only trigger that chooses among a precomputed low-dimensional graph bank, has bounded calibration/cost and false-alarm behavior, and improves unconditional utility over strong pooled static, trailing-window, EWMA and periodic policies on fresh streams. The studied 45-feature map, fixed gate/readout assumptions, d=3 geometry and across-shot (not within-shot) drift remain Q-SHIFT-specific limitations.

**Monitored source review:** arXiv primary-source HTML checked 2026-11-14; no repository data accessed. No tests, simulation, sampling, decoder inference, training, seeds or protected access.

### Stein et al., calibration-conditioned FiLM neural decoder
- **Citation/source:** Samuel Stein et al., “Calibration-Conditioned FiLM Decoders for Low-Latency Decoding of Quantum Error Correction Evaluated on IBM Repetition-Code Experiments,” arXiv:2601.16123 (22 Jan 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16123 and https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16123.
- **Primary sections reviewed:** abstract/introduction, architecture, training objective/protocol, evaluation, generalization, latency and appendix architecture table.
- **Method/information:** A three-layer GCN encodes hardware-graph features (including T1/T2, readout-assignment and gate-error calibration); generated FiLM conditioning modulates a CNN on syndrome tiles. Trained per `(distance, rounds, basis)` on 70/30 data with supervised per-qubit correction targets from final prepared/measured states. Heavy conditioning is asynchronous; weight-folding is used to keep the stated decoding path comparable in complexity to the unconditioned CNN.
- **Evidence:** Reports 2,760,704 IBM repetition-code shots and 400 calibration snapshots over three devices, with a later evaluation on recalibrated/unseen qubit chains acquired about a week after training. The abstract reports up to 11.1x LER reduction relative to hardware-informed MWPM at its largest `d=11,r=11` setting. This is a paper-reported result, not independently reproduced here.
- **Relevance/limits:** Strong example of adaptive deployment using explicit calibration telemetry plus a trained syndrome-to-correction model. It is not a detector-only burst-parameter estimator, uses logical correction supervision during training, targets 1D repetition codes, and does not establish that Q-SHIFT's 45-feature stream can support the same inference. It belongs in an adjacent “telemetry + supervised model” category and cannot be used as evidence for Q-SHIFT's no-training controller.

**Novelty update:** Distinguish four adjacent adaptation paradigms in any final synthesis: (1) syndrome-derived parametric rate tracking (e.g., graph-edge windows), (2) model-mismatch/confidence postselection (`W` plus gap), (3) hardware-telemetry-conditioned supervised decoders (FiLM), and (4) pseudo-label reweighting (DGR). A Q-SHIFT contribution would need to specify that it is in (1), with a narrow categorical burst model and a prospective graph-switch policy; general “adaptive decoding under drift” is not a novel claim.

### Deterministic mapping of the published edge-moment estimator to Q-SHIFT

The equation for a graphlike bulk-edge probability in Bhardwaj et al., “Adaptive Estimation of Drifting Noise in Quantum Error Correction,” was applied to the five detector pairs generated by Q-SHIFT's five independent burst sites. This is a deterministic population calculation using the published marginal/cofire inversion, not a sampled estimator result. Across the existing 9×9 `(p_burst,p_measurement)` grid (`p_burst=.002… .018`, `p_measurement=.001… .005`), all 405 exact-categorical pair estimates were valid and recovered the model's common burst probability with mean absolute error `4.18e-17` and maximum `1.65e-16`. The 405 estimates from the approximate DEM also recovered p to floating-point accuracy (maximum absolute error `3.47e-16`). Under an additional 3×3 exact-circuit sensitivity over gate p `.002,.003,.004` and burst p `.002,.010,.018` at measurement p `.003`, all 45 pair estimates recovered p with maximum absolute error `5.38e-17`. The population identities were independently checked against direct XOR recursion with maximum feature-mean error `6.53e-15`.

**Interpretation:** this is unusually strong structural compatibility evidence for the published edge-moment formula under the declared Q-SHIFT categorical circuit, including the explored independent measurement/scalar-gate changes and two existing alternative Pauli-bias regimes (`x_biased`, `z_biased`). The latter are deterministic model-family sensitivity conditions; they do not alter or replace the pinned balanced primary configuration. It suggests a very small detector-only edge-rate estimator should be included as a first-line comparator to the 45-feature profile. It does not establish finite-history bias/variance, alarm false positives, robustness to correlated readout with matching detector masks, drift delay, or decoder utility. It is an application of an existing method, not a novelty claim. Reproduction source/result: `published_edge_moment_inversion_audit.py` and `PUBLISHED_EDGE_MOMENT_INVERSION_AUDIT.json`.

The complete single-fault map gives a structural reason: for each target pair, no nonburst categorical alternative flips both detectors, and no nonburst categorical site has different mutually exclusive alternatives that can flip each detector separately. This was checked over all 1,974 mapped nonzero alternatives in `BURST_PAIR_JOINT_SUPPORT_AUDIT.json`; each of the five target pairs has zero nonburst alternatives and zero nonburst site-groups touching both. Under the model's independent physical-site assumption, the burst site is therefore the sole common-cause contribution to each pair covariance. This supports model-conditional cause identification beyond mere profile fit. An external nuisance that shares detector masks remains exactly aliased by construction, so the result does not establish hardware-level cause identification.

At the nominal point, an exact-covariance iid delta-method calculation for the unweighted average of the five edge estimates gives an asymptotic SD coefficient `0.04714 / sqrt(N)`; the existing two-parameter 45-feature profile moment calculation gives a burst-coordinate coefficient `0.05085 / sqrt(N)` under its own locally linearized objective. At the lower burst boundary `p_b=.002` and nominal measurement `p_m=.003`, the edge-average coefficient is `0.02370/sqrt(N)`, corresponding to roughly 1,264 independent shots for a normal-approximation separation of three standard errors from zero. At this low p, an unbounded normal approximation gives an 11.6% chance of a negative raw estimate for a 200-shot window, versus 0.38% for 1,000 shots; clipping at zero would add an approximate `9.53e-5` bias at N=200. These are asymptotic tail/clipping heuristics, not exact finite-sample probabilities, formal power, an approved window size, or interval coverage. Coefficients assume known population covariance and iid shots; the methods estimate different parameter sets. Reproduction: `edge_moment_delta_method.py` and `EDGE_MOMENT_DELTA_METHOD.json`; analytic gradient/Hessian checks against finite differences were within `3.1e-10` and `2.5e-10`.

### Learned syndrome-statistics noise model and neural predecoder (important adjacent prior art)
- Chamberland, Olle, Li, Thornton & Baratta, “Fast and accurate AI-based pre-decoders for surface codes,” arXiv:2604.12841v1 (14 Apr 2026), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12841v1). The second component is especially adjacent to data-driven decoder-weight calibration and uses no explicit circuit model at inference, though its training uses generated examples with known noise models.

The learned 25-parameter model is a static/current-batch weight inferencer, not established streaming change detection, and its edge/hyperedge formulas are tied to the paper's surface-code noise family and decoded DEM representation. A fair future comparison would need separate compute/data budgets and information parity; no training or comparison was done here.
- Implementation provenance: official [NVIDIA/Ising-Decoding](https://github.com/NVIDIA/ising-decoding) repository, snapshot of `main` observed at full commit `12bbeb13c5cfc1a064171ac91b07aea61f2eaae8` on 14 Nov 2026 via read-only `git ls-remote`. The code is Apache-2.0, but Hugging Face checkpoints are separately licensed/access-controlled and were not downloaded. No source was cloned, installed, trained or run.
The checked methods now distinguish (i) estimating probabilities of known independent DEM events or aggregates (Blume-Kohout/Young; Takou/Brown; Remm et al.), (ii) topology discovery and model augmentation (CAHR; Schwartzman-Nowik et al.), (iii) choosing parameters to improve decoding from labeled LER (Sivak et al.; Pancotti et al.), (iv) causal/temporal rate tracking (Bhardwaj et al.; Spitz et al.; DGR), and (v) per-shot compatibility/postselection (Hoyt et al.). ### 2026 Mamba/state-space neural decoder publication-status correction
The earlier entry records arXiv:2510.22724v1 because that was the reviewed source at that time. As of 14 Nov 2026, the work has a peer-reviewed early-access publication: Lee, Hur & Park, “Scalable neural decoders for practical real-time quantum error correction,” *npj Quantum Information* (published 28 Oct 2026), DOI `10.1038/s41534-026-01403-y`, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-026-01403-y. The paper reports Mamba complexity `O(d^2)` versus its Transformer comparator's `O(d^4)`, matching Transformer performance on the cited Sycamore memory set, and simulated latency-injected threshold values 0.0104 vs 0.0097. Its Sycamore model was pretrained on up to 100M synthetic samples and then fine-tuned on half of the hardware dataset; real-time runs used separate synthetic SI1000 training. Thus it is decoder architecture/latency prior art, not detector-only causal estimation. The latency-injected comparisons are simulations and not Q-SHIFT results; code/license pin was not inspected.

### Evidence boundary for logical-risk variational TN learning
The earlier Pancotti et al. entry remains a preprint-level source, not a detector-only method. The full text explicitly uses observed logical-observable outcomes in a binary cross-entropy objective; its reported static d=3/r=3 optimization uses 500 iterations × 30,000 labeled samples per batch, and its drift experiment uses fresh sampled labeled batches along a Brownian-logit parameter path. The paper presents exact TN contractions for its tractable d=3 setting, but states exact-contraction cost grows with graph treewidth and d=5 was outside that study. This remains a high-information offline/online supervised track, not an information-parity comparator to Q-SHIFT's detector-only controller.

**Checkpoint 95 taxonomy refinement:** the “detector-event RL physical-control” category must be separate from (a) detector-only physical-parameter estimation, (b) LER-supervised decoder-prior steering, and (c) supervised per-shot neural decoding. Sivak et al. demonstrate the first on hardware and report complementary decoder steering separately; Pancotti et al. use logical labels in a differentiable TN loss; Mamba is a supervised decoder/latency architecture. Their actuators and information sets differ and their gains are not transferable to Q-SHIFT.

### Composite unknown-post-change QCD (2026-11-14)
Reviewed three primary sources on window-limited composite quickest detection. Lee & Xie (arXiv:2610.16616) provide a GLM-specific VI-WLCUSUM with predictable scoring, valid-density/common-support assumptions for false-alarm control, and additional finite-window estimation, curvature, optimization-error and moment assumptions for delay bounds. Liang & Veeravalli (arXiv:2311.15128) provide nonparametric window-limited methods under density smoothness/convergence assumptions; that route is not presently motivated for Q-SHIFT's sparse discrete outcome vectors with a low-dimensional physical law. Liang, Tartakovsky & Veeravalli (arXiv:2110.01581) address structured nonstationary post-change distributions and discuss dependent extensions; smooth Q-SHIFT drift requires an explicit trajectory class and assumption mapping. Applicability note and direct sources are in `COMPOSITE_QCD_LITERATURE_NOTE.md`. No off-the-shelf false-alarm or delay guarantee is claimed for estimated pre-change/noise nuisance, DEM approximation, arbitrary smooth drift, or decoder benefit.
