# Drift-window time-axis audit

## Question

Can published syndrome-edge sliding-window formulas be transferred to Q-SHIFT's stream of independently reset five-round shots, and what does the “relative window” update mean on that stream?

## Primary-source method and time unit

Bhardwaj et al. define a time index over **syndrome-extraction cycles in an ongoing QEC memory process**. Their fixed-window estimator is the temporal average of the edge probability in the preceding `W` cycles, under their Markovian time-dependent Pauli-noise model. The paper's relative-window construction subtracts two overlapping population averages of lengths `W` and `W+1`; in expectation, the one additional time point is isolated. Their spectral cutoff and drift-period statements are expressed in QEC cycles, not independent simulator jobs. See the paper's sliding-window Eqs. (3)–(9), relative-window Eq. (11), and Table I: [PRX Quantum 7, 033024 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1hc-nqw5), [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09491).

The Q-SHIFT source examined here instead builds a five-round circuit execution with one burst probability shared across its five independent `CORRELATED_ERROR` boundary events, then treats each execution as a completed shot. The current research question is therefore **drift between independently reset shots**, with one 45-feature vector per completed shot. Its stream index is shot number. It is not valid to concatenate round 1–5 of one shot with rounds 1–5 of the next and call that one ongoing-memory cycle series without a different physical protocol and detector construction.

## Causal step response on the shot axis

If the burst rate steps from `p0` to `p1` between completed shots, a trailing window of width `W` used before predicting the next shot begins with only pre-change data. After `n` post-change shots have been observed, for `0≤n≤W`, the population window estimate is

`p_hat_n = p0 + (n/W)(p1-p0)`.

Only after `W` new shots does the population average equal `p1`; the decoder can change for the next shot after each update, but the first post-change shot necessarily uses the prior graph if no change is known in advance. This is deterministic lag, separate from detector-history noise and any alarm threshold.

For an EWMA updated after each observed shot, `m_n = (1-λ)m_(n-1)+λ p1`, so `m_n = p1+(p0-p1)(1-λ)^n` after the step. Its response has an exponential tail instead of the finite `W`-shot linear ramp. These equations do not establish which smoother minimizes LER: decoder-optimal p may differ from physical p, and no logical-outcome study is authorized. Fair later comparisons should match the same pre-shot information and tune `W`, `λ`, trigger thresholds, and periodic cadence only on eligible development streams.

## Deterministic transfer check

For a fixed declared background model, each exact categorical-circuit detector-feature expectation is affine in the shot's burst probability for this known-support Bernoulli pair mechanism. I formed convex mixtures of those per-shot population means to represent windows containing two burst-rate regimes, then applied the published pair-edge moment inversion to the pooled moments. Across 15 low/high-rate and mixture-weight cases (`p` in `{0.002, 0.010, 0.018}`; 75 edge estimates total), it recovered the arithmetic mean burst probability in the window. Maximum absolute bias was `6.94e-17`; the largest spread among the five edge estimates within a case was `5.55e-17`. This is deterministic population algebra on the exact categorical circuit, independently separated from the approximate DEM result. Reproduction: `shot_window_mixture_audit.py`; output: `SHOT_WINDOW_MIXTURE_AUDIT.json`.

This supports a limited mapping: a trailing window over completed shots can estimate the **window-average** `p_burst` in the current static-background/shotwise-drift model. It does not estimate the current shot's instantaneous rate without lag or noise, and does not establish finite-history accuracy. The identity relies on an unchanged background detector distribution and the current known two-detector burst support. Drift in measurement/gate composition, within-shot burst probability changes, cross-shot dependence, or a same-mask correlated-readout nuisance is outside this check.

An additional independent-nonidentical-shot delta-method calculation propagates the exact one-shot categorical feature covariance through the pooled inversion. For a 50/50 low/high window at `p=.002/.018`, the asymptotic SD coefficient is `0.0470111/sqrt(W)`; the stationary `p=.010` coefficient is `0.0471444/sqrt(W)`. For 25/75 and 75/25 windows, the coefficients are `0.0550777/sqrt(W)` and `0.0372302/sqrt(W)`, compared with stationary-at-the-same-mean coefficients `0.0551630/sqrt(W)` and `0.0373563/sqrt(W)`. Pooled and stationary population feature means agree within `8.33e-17` in the tested cases. These small differences arise in the asymptotic variance under the declared model; they are not evidence of finite-window accuracy, coverage, or robust tracking. Reproduction: `shot_window_delta_method.py`; output: `SHOT_WINDOW_DELTA_METHOD.json`.

## Exact finite-count diagnostic for one pair

To expose finite-window behavior without sampling, `exact_edge_multinomial_audit.py` exhaustively sums the exact four-cell multinomial distribution for detector pair `(D8,D10)` at `p={.002,.010,.018}` and `W={25,50,100,200}`. It enumerates up to 1,373,701 count tables; mass sums to one, enumerated feature means match multinomial moments within `4.0e-15`, and the zero-cofire probability matches `(1-P11)^W` within tolerance. For `p=.002,W=200`, the raw single-edge plug-in inverse is negative with probability `0.5898`, while the probability of seeing no pair cofires is `0.6069`; for `p=.010,W=200`, these are `0.1281` and `0.1316`; for `p=.018,W=200`, `0.02749` and `0.02821`. Invalid-real-domain probability was below `2.0e-74` at `W=200` in these cases, but negative, physically inadmissible point estimates remain common, especially when burst cofires are rare. Conditional RMSE among valid real outputs at `W=200` was `.003745`, `.007460`, and `.009829` for the three rates, respectively.

These are exact count-distribution probabilities under the fixed-rate categorical pair model, not Monte Carlo. They are a **single edge only**: the five Q-SHIFT edge estimates share detector-history structure, so these probabilities cannot be transferred to their equal-weight average. They do show why the IID delta-method normal approximation is inadequate for a rare-event pair at modest history lengths and why boundary handling cannot be left implicit. No clipping rule or history length is selected.

## Bounded pair-likelihood alternative

I also enumerated the exact finite-count distribution of a detector-only multinomial MLE using the same four pair counts and exact categorical endpoint probabilities. Its model is `q(p)=(1-p)q0+p q1`; `q0` is extrapolated from two positive-p exact profiles and `q1` is the deterministic both-bit complement implied by the known burst support. The affine relation was checked against the third rate with maximum cell-probability error `1.11e-16`. The likelihood is maximized on the conventional probability range `[0,.5]` using a deterministic bisection of its concave score.

At `W=200`, its bias/RMSE were `+.000380/.003470` at `p=.002`, `+.0000825/.007327` at `.010`, and `+.0000177/.009779` at `.018`. It lands exactly at zero with the same probabilities as the no-cofire event in this enumeration (`.6069/.1316/.02821`). Compared with the raw moment inverse's conditional RMSE, the single-pair MLE's RMSE is lower by about 7%, 1.8%, and 0.5%, respectively; this is a modest, model-conditioned numerical difference, not a statistical comparison of decoder performance. It removes negative estimates by constraining the parameter and exhibits the corresponding boundary mass. This single-pair result does not establish an advantage for the five-edge composite likelihood, whose pair observations are dependent within each shot. The required exact model probabilities also make its calibration/model-information assumption stronger than a generic detector-only moment fit. Reproduction: `exact_edge_likelihood_audit.py`; output: `EXACT_EDGE_LIKELIHOOD_AUDIT.json`.

## Five-pair composite-score dependence

For a dependence-aware asymptotic diagnostic, I computed the exact joint distribution of the ten detector bits in the five disjoint burst pairs via parity recursion, then evaluated the composite marginal-likelihood score. The exact Godambe variance is `J/H^2`, where `H` is expected negative score derivative and `J` is the variance of the *joint per-shot sum* of the five edge scores. If one incorrectly assumes independent edge scores, J is understated by 1.96%, 0.488%, and 0.283% at burst p `.002,.010,.018`; the resulting asymptotic SD coefficients are `.0235755`, `.0470801`, `.0620472` per `sqrt(W)` versus `.0233473`, `.0469656`, `.0619598` under the false independence assumption. Pair marginals independently match the 45-feature exact circuit means within `3.3e-15`. This shows dependence is measurable but modest for this *specific asymptotic exact-model score*. It does not validate a finite-window composite MLE, boundary behavior, nuisance robustness, or adaptation. Reproduction: `composite_edge_likelihood_sensitivity.py`; output: `COMPOSITE_EDGE_LIKELIHOOD_SENSITIVITY.json`.

The exact joint distribution also gives the probability that no one of the five pairs cofires in a single shot: `.98782/.95072/.91476` at p `.002/.010/.018`. Under IID shots, the chance of zero pair cofires anywhere in a 200-shot window is `.08625`, `4.08e-5`, and `1.82e-8`. This is lower than the corresponding one-pair zero-cofire probability because five edges contribute information. It is only a sparsity diagnostic; it is not the probability that a five-edge estimate hits its lower boundary.

## Per-shot information for detecting burst-rate changes

Using exact parity recursion for the joint distribution of the ten detectors in the five burst pairs, I computed per-shot KL and total-variation distances between selected old/new burst rates. Under equal measurement p `.003`, the exact-circuit KL `D(P_new||P_old)` is `.03480` nats/shot for `.002→.010`, `.01197` for `.010→.018`, and `.10228` for `.002→.018`. The corresponding approximate-DEM KL values are `.03480`, `.01197`, and `.10230`. These are information in the ten-detector subset; the full 40-detector record may contain more information, while nuisance-robust estimation may require using it differently.

Over the coarse independent old/new measurement grid `{.001,.003,.005}`, the minimum exact-circuit KLs are `.03367`, `.01172`, and `.09909`, attained with old/new measurement p both `.005`. Approximate-DEM minima are within `2.0e-5` nats/shot. This suggests that the tested measurement range does not erase the pair-subset signal for these burst steps under the declared model. It is not a calibrated change detector, a false-alarm guarantee, or an expected detection delay; thresholds, parameter estimation, and sequential stopping rules remain unsettled. I separately mixed exact per-shot profiles across measurement probabilities `.001` and `.005`, keeping circuit-level categorical and approximate-DEM calculations distinct. With burst p fixed at `.002`, `.010`, or `.018`, equal measurement mixtures shift the pooled equal-edge inversion by `+2.50e-5`, `+2.46e-5`, or `+2.42e-5`, with between-edge spreads around `4.3e-5`. When burst and measurement rates change together across shots, equal-edge biases are `-5.27e-5` for low/low to high/high pairing and `+1.018e-4` for low/high to high/low pairing. The corrected equal-weight nine-condition mixture across `{.002,.010,.018}×{.001,.003,.005}` has arithmetic mean burst p `.010` and bias `+1.64e-5`. Across the corrected scenarios, approximate-DEM and exact-circuit edge-estimate biases differ by at most `4.51e-17`.

## Relative-window feasibility on one-vector-per-shot data

The paper's relative-window population identity is algebraically portable only if Q-SHIFT defines its window statistic on the shot axis and its moment assumptions continue to hold. But its empirical differencing has a serious granularity issue here. If `W` and `W+1` sample averages are computed from nested windows of one vector per shot, then `(W+1)*mean(W+1) - W*mean(W)` reduces exactly to the single newest shot vector. The averaging that made the statistics precise cancels. The five edge-moment inversion then operates on one noisy vector's coordinates; its raw covariances can be outside the real inversion domain and have no established useful variance. The original paper's theoretical population identity does not imply a low-noise per-shot Q-SHIFT estimator.

Accordingly:

- Treat **trailing-window inversion** as a candidate estimator of average recent burst rate, with separately chosen lag/window length and finite-history validation.
- Treat **EWMA** as an alternative smoother with an explicit effective history and lag.
- Treat **relative-window differencing** as a theoretical comparator only; do not promote it to a candidate controller until a deterministic finite-history analysis establishes its variance, domain failure rate, and dependence assumptions.
- For abrupt changes, a causal chart may trigger an update, but it should be compared with simple trailing-window, EWMA, and periodic policies at equal detector-information and graph-cache budgets. A current-shot decision must be frozen before using that shot's detectors.

## Research interpretation and novelty

The new mapping adds model-specific compatibility to the prior art, not generic novelty: published syndrome-only windows already estimate temporal-average event rates in an ongoing memory process. The Q-SHIFT-specific claim, if any, would be the algebraic known-burst-support inversion and a useful causal policy over independently reset shots. The deterministic mixture check makes a shot-window mean plausible under the declared model; it provides no evidence of adaptation benefit, false-alarm rate, delay, or logical-error change.

## Reproducibility and integrity

The deterministic audit uses Stim's noiseless `reference_sample` only to map Pauli faults, then exact independent categorical parity-character products to compute population means. It makes zero sampler calls and uses no logical outcomes, decoder, seed, protected material, or experimental data. The exact circuit calculation is distinguished from the approximate DEM in the audit result. Historical research documents and artifacts remain unchanged except for current working notes and this new audit package.

## Checkpoint 125 — bounded e-factor on the completed-shot axis

The newer robust scalar e-factor uses the same shot-level statistic `X_t`, the average of five pair-cofire indicators. It is a **regime alarm**, not a burst-rate estimator. Under the current decoder-only actuator, the circuit emits shot t independently of which cached decoder graph was selected; the controller may compute the e-factor only after observing shot t and can affect decoding beginning at shot t+1. This preserves the causal boundary and avoids same-shot leakage, but it does not remove the unavoidable first-post-change shot lag.

The e-factor product may summarize all eligible prior detector evidence, unlike a width-W point estimator. Its guarantee depends on the conditional source law remaining inside its current low- or high-regime rectangle; it does not estimate `p_b`, select an intermediate graph, or make the middle burst band `(.002,.010)` actionable. A later comparison must give trailing-window, EWMA, periodic and e-factor policies the same pre-shot detector history and cached graph bank, and must tune all policy thresholds only on eligible development streams. Relative-window differencing remains a theoretical comparator at this granularity because it collapses to one newest shot vector.
