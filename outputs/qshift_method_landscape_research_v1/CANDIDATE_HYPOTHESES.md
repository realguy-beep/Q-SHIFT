# Candidate hypotheses — initial falsifiable questions

1. **Causal event-triggered recalibration.** Detector-only change detection may reduce regret versus an equally informed periodic update when abrupt regimes persist long enough to amortize construction and switching costs. Falsified if it fails to beat matched periodic and pooled-fixed arms, or causes unacceptable stationary/false-alarm harm.
2. **Uncertainty-gated no-switch policy.** An online controller that retains the incumbent unless detector-only evidence crosses a calibrated uncertainty threshold may limit harmful switches under ambiguous drift. Falsified if it offers no net benefit over a fixed incumbent plus the same estimator, or if thresholds require labels/future data.
3. **Noise-regime-conditional BeliefMatching value.** BM may differ from PyMatching only when identifiable graphlike correlations or a supported hypergraph structure are present. Falsified for tested regimes if paired predictions/errors offer no practically meaningful advantage at matched assumed-model information and acceptable cost.
4. **Robust static mixture/ensemble.** A small ensemble or robust static configuration may protect against uncertain model selection without causal adaptation. Falsified if its resource-adjusted worst-case performance is no better than a strong single static model.

These are hypotheses for literature and feasibility assessment, not planned experiments or claims.

## Hypothesis refinement after primary-source review

**H1 — Detector-only causal trigger + low-dimensional cached graph bank is testable, not established.** Prior Q-SHIFT development indicates static calibration captures most of the burst-conditioned gain and measured adaptive-vs-pooled-fixed difference was small. A future test must ask whether trigger logic reduces worst-regime harm without paying false-alarm cost under stationary or measurement-mismatched conditions. Falsify if it cannot beat a strong static policy by a predeclared practical margin over plausible drift schedules, or if equal-information trailing/EWMA/periodic approaches match it at lower cost.

The novelty boundary is narrower after reviewing Bhardwaj et al. (2026): syndrome-only sliding-window estimation and adaptive decoding under drifting Pauli noise are already published. A Q-SHIFT-specific contribution would need to demonstrate a materially distinct, identifiable known-mechanism burst estimator or a useful trigger/controller result beyond equally informed windows and periodic updates; “detector-only adaptation” alone is not new.

**H2 — Matching-history reweighting may be data-hungry and self-reinforcing.** DGR is a concrete published path but uses MWPM matchings as proxy error events. Q-SHIFT would need to test estimator bias under burst/measurement confounding and intervention rules that prevent a bad graph from causing its own confirmation. Falsify if short histories are biased/unstable or no net benefit remains after decoding/runtime costs.

**H3 — Correlation-aware decoding adds value only if the representation conveys correlation information.** The Q-SHIFT selected graphlike pair-X event produced identical PyMatching graph/predictions under correlation flag on/off in a prior bounded plumbing check. For other mechanisms, any comparison must separate decomposition/DEM information from algorithm flags and preserve information parity. Falsify the added method as a distinct comparator if matched input produces identical graph and predictions.

**H4 — A simple sequential detector may be enough; statistical guarantees need model-specific calibration.** CUSUM is interpretable for abrupt change, adaptive windows for gradual drift, and BOCPD for explicit regime persistence, but independent scalar assumptions are not established for 45 detector bits/pair features. Falsify the need for a sophisticated detector if a prespecified EWMA/trailing-window schedule provides similar delay and false-alarm control.

**H5 — Better mean-feature identifiability does not imply better finite-history decisions.** Historical deterministic identifiability findings and the existing small paired adaptive-vs-pooled effect require separate analysis of estimator variance, dwell/hysteresis and cost. A method should advance only with a causal information set, bounded switch budget, and per-condition and pooled paired outcomes in a fresh development-only comparison.

**Estimator-literature consequence:** the sophisticated alternatives now include (1) edge-covariance inversion with independent graph events, (2) broader model-likelihood Bayesian inference using tensor-network likelihoods and MCMC/SMC, and (3) Gaussian-process prediction from approximate rates. None can be dropped into the current Q-SHIFT estimator unchanged: the injected X8/X10 event is Bernoulli and independent across round boundaries, but its graph-signature mapping and separation from categorical baseline gate-noise alternatives still need an explicit likelihood or identifiability derivation; the shot/round time axis must also match the proposed controller. A low-dimensional fitted profile bank with a separately designed trigger remains the simpler candidate; Bayesian fitting is a reference design if deterministic exact population likelihoods and compute can be established, not the primary implement-now choice.

**Provisional novelty note:** prior work already covers syndrome-derived adaptive weights, Gaussian-process tracking, DGR graph reweighting, sliding-window edge estimation, Bayesian SMC and neural continual predecoding. Q-SHIFT cannot claim novelty at the broad “adapt decoder to drift” level. Possible contribution, if demonstrated, is a narrowly controlled detector-only causal trigger/switch policy for drift in the known-support X8/X10 Bernoulli pair-event rate, with measurement nuisance, plus equal-information static/periodic comparisons and cost accounting. Existing d=3 synthetic development alone does not support broad generality or strong publication forecasts.

## Newly surfaced hypotheses and boundaries

6. **Known-event estimator vs blind DEM discovery.** If the synthetic pair-burst topology is known, a low-dimensional burst/measurement fit may require far less information than topology discovery. Falsify the need for the simpler model only if a declared unknown-mechanism setting cannot be represented by the source circuit/DEM family and an authorized deterministic/experimental design shows added identifiable structure.
7. **Direct LER prior fitting vs detector-only estimation.** Labeled offline policy tuning (RL/PPO-style or differentiable TN objectives) may choose decoder parameters better than a physical-rate estimator because its target is logical error directly. It requires label-containing development allocations, no evaluation leakage, and a separately declared information budget. It cannot be represented as a detector-only online controller.
8. **Prior-update integrity.** A controller can be harmed by either physical false alarms or a corrupted calibration-to-graph update path. Hashes, immutable cache keys, bounded update deltas and fail-closed fallback are engineering safeguards. A malicious-prior threat model requires a distinct security evaluation and is not evidence of natural drift benefit.
9. **DEM topology expansion.** CAHR and data-driven augmentation provide plausible routes if the model omits real multi-detector mechanisms. In Q-SHIFT's current known X8X10 synthetic burst, mechanism discovery would be needless complexity; its utility is contingent on a different, explicitly declared unknown-noise objective.
10. **Coarse detector-fire monitoring may be useful as a fail-safe without identifying the cause.** ReloQate shows a system-level use for recent detector-fire rate: predict conservative logical risk and trigger a hardware remap. Q-SHIFT could analogously use a predeclared residual/alarm to fall back to a fixed graph when the profile is unsupported, but this does not imply p_burst identifiability or graph-switch benefit. Falsify this fallback signal if it does not predict a relevant failure/risk condition at a tolerable false-alarm rate; evaluate it separately from causal parameter attribution and unconditional decoder LER.
11. **Known-mechanism causal tracking may be simpler than general neural noise learning, but that distinction must be demonstrated.** Chamberland et al. directly infer surface-code matching probabilities from detector statistics using a trained model and rich edge/hyperedge formulas. Q-SHIFT's proposed advantage is not generic detector-only weight estimation; it would have to show that fitting its known per-boundary Bernoulli X8/X10 burst rate and switching cached graphs is accurate, cheaper, or more causally reliable within the full circuit's categorical baseline channels. Falsify the need for the specialized controller if an information-matched, sufficiently lightweight static/windowed syndrome-to-weight estimator matches it at lower deployment cost.
12. **The published edge inversion is an exact physical burst-p estimator only under the mapped known-support model.** Deterministic support enumeration found no nonburst categorical alternative or same-site mutually exclusive group that can toggle both detectors in any target pair; this explains exact population recovery in the declared balanced circuit. A hypothetical independent nuisance with exactly the same detector mask remains observationally aliased, and physical cause claims cannot generalize beyond the modeled circuit. Further, the raw plug-in inverse is finite-history inadmissible below zero frequently for one edge (59.0% at p=.002,W=200); a bounded pair likelihood trades that pathology for zero-boundary mass under stronger known-model assumptions. Require a declared boundary-aware candidate and full five-edge uncertainty before any controller use.

### Candidate H6 — mixture e-alarm for one predeclared directional change
For streams with a trusted initial burst regime and at most one action-triggering change, use a fixed summable prior over candidate change times and the already certified bounded scalar e-factor. A telescoping prior permits an O(1) time/memory update and preserves a single global false-alarm bound under the corresponding null. The trigger remains separate from any detector-only p estimate or graph-selection rule; action begins on the next shot, and post-trigger behavior is hold/lock pending external authorization. Test this candidate only against equal-information static/trailing/EWMA controls if a future owner-approved study specifies the no-change law, direction, alpha, indifference-band behavior, and graph action in advance. It is not a bidirectional autonomous controller, and no decoder utility is established.

### Candidate H6 refinement — alarm evidence may appear before endpoint regimes
Checkpoint 134 shows that the fixed directional mean e-factor can have certified positive drift in part of the nominal `.002–.010` indifference band. This is evidence about detector-law departure under the exact model, not evidence that the high- or low-end graph is optimal at those burst rates. Any future controller must predefine a hold/abstain or calibrated intermediate action; otherwise an earlier alarm can make the policy less appropriate even if statistically valid. Smooth transitions through uncertified subbands remain open.

### Candidate H6 ramp qualification
A one-change mixture can support a mathematical detection bound for specified linear burst ramps by starting evidence at the first point with certified positive log drift and adding a transparent candidate-time prior penalty. The checkpoint-135 dwell numbers are highly conditional on ramp slope, starting shot, nuisance rectangles and endpoint persistence. Treat them as protocol-planning sensitivities only; do not generalize them to arbitrary drift or use them as expected runtime/detection results.

### Candidate H6 episode limitation
A telescoping alpha allocation can bound false switches across externally re-baselined one-change episodes, but only when each new baseline/null is independently established. Without that requirement, the opposite-direction e-process may be run outside its null and the guarantee collapses. This is a safe review option for a future human-in-the-loop protocol, not autonomous bidirectional adaptation.

### Candidate H7 — predeclared lambda bank for unknown change magnitude
Use a fixed convex mixture over a small set of lambdas, each tuned analytically to a distinct burst region, and combine each with the same unknown-start prior. This remains null-valid without online parameter fitting and extends positive drift certificates through more of the intermediate range. The state cost is linear in bank size; false-alarm thresholds include the lambda prior weight. Compare this against one fixed lambda and equal-information static/trailing/EWMA baselines only in a later authorized study. The output still cannot map an alarm to a decoder graph, and upward/downward nulls remain separate.
