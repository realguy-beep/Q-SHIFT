
## Tentative conservative architecture for a future separately authorized study

1. **Keep a strong frozen PyMatching policy as the default.** Include the strongest detector-selected static configuration (and its explicit selection budget) so an adaptive policy must show value beyond pooled-static choice.
2. **Cache a finite graph bank.** Build every candidate on frozen DEM templates in advance; no graph construction on the causal path. Keep generation identity, decoder model identity, and assumed-noise provenance separate.
3. **Compare a direct edge-moment estimate with the 45-feature profile before selecting a controller input.** The published independent-edge inversion deterministically recovers Q-SHIFT's common burst p from each of its five detector pairs at the population level, including explored measurement/scalar-gate changes and existing `x_biased`/`z_biased` categorical gate regimes. Its iid delta-method burst SD coefficient at nominal is `0.04714/sqrt(N)` versus `0.05085/sqrt(N)` for the joint 45-feature profile's burst coordinate, but this is only a conditional asymptotic comparison and estimates different parameter sets. This is a strong reason to include it in a bounded estimator-feasibility study, not to declare it superior with finite histories. Preserve the 45-feature profile as a matched comparator until uncertainty, mismatch alarms, and decoder utility are compared. Do not silently interpret an unsupported channel as burst p. A residual is a mismatch indication, not physical cause attribution: under the matched-mask correlated-readout stress in `CORRELATED_READOUT_IDENTIFIABILITY_NOTE.md`, burst and nuisance probabilities are exactly aliased to the detector-only estimator.
4. **Separate state estimation from change detection.** State estimate: trailing window of complete previous shots, profile fit, with an uncertainty/residual measure. Trigger: a prespecified vector score or likelihood-ratio/control-chart statistic calibrated on prior eligible development streams. Do not tune alarm thresholds on evaluation streams.
5. **Make every action prospective.** The estimate used for shot `t` may consume detectors through shot `t-1` only. Hysteresis, minimum dwell, maximum switches per horizon, and no-change fallback are frozen. Predictions for shot `t` are made before its detectors are available to the update.
6. **Use matched baselines.** Same detector observations and cached bank for fixed, trailing-window, EWMA, periodic-recalibration, and trigger policies. Each method gets the same feature/parameter family and development-only tuning budget. Report true-p only as privileged reference.
7. **Gate advancement on practical value and costs.** A fresh development-only pilot should first estimate compute, adaptation delay, false switches, calibration burden, and paired utility versus strong pooled-static. A future full study requires a separate freeze/authorization after that review. No performance gain is presumed.

This architecture is a design hypothesis only. It does not satisfy or authorize execution.

### Model-compatibility score is not an adaptation rule
Recent work on decoder model compatibility makes minimum-cost tail diagnostics a plausible detector-only signal for *records* that are poorly supported by a frozen DEM. It does not imply that the controller can attribute that residual to burst probability, measurement flips, or any particular physical cause. In Q-SHIFT, keep three questions separate: (i) does the profile fit the observed detector-feature mean? (ii) does a record/history appear unlikely under the current model? (iii) would a new cached matching graph reduce unconditional logical risk? Each requires a different statistic and evidence. If online outputs cannot be rejected, conditional postselection gains are operationally inapplicable; if a residual guard is used, it should first yield to a fixed fallback when outside the supported feature family, not force an unsupported parameter estimate. Neither a residual threshold nor a parameter switch can be called calibrated from the papers alone.

**Current provisional ranking after literature update:** first, deterministic model-adequacy/identifiability for the exact categorical circuit and approximate DEM templates; second, only if that passes, a shot-indexed trailing-window estimate using the published pair-moment inversion (or the existing profile as comparator), then a simple causal controller with cached graphs, benchmarked against strong pooled static plus EWMA/periodic. The deterministic two-regime population mixture now supports recovery of the arithmetic window-mean burst rate under the current static-background model. It does not validate finite-history performance. The published relative-window difference is not promoted: on one feature vector per independently reset shot, nested-window differencing algebraically leaves one noisy shot. Third, consider telemetry-conditioned/supervised or postselection directions only if Q-SHIFT scope expands. This ordering is a feasibility recommendation, not evidence of utility or novelty.

**Deterministic progress, with narrow scope:** an independent-event calculation over the current approximate DEM produced a rank-2 local mean-feature Jacobian for burst/measurement rates at `(0.010,0.003)` and unique exact detector supports for all five burst edges at the documented DEM configuration. In that local model, the five pair-cofire features respond strongly to burst p (derivatives about 0.949–0.956) and weakly to measurement p (about 0.059–0.085), while other detector marginals are more measurement-sensitive. This advances the DEM-level structural question only. An exact categorical-circuit 45-feature expectation calculation now agrees with direct XOR recursion to 3.84e-15 at nominal settings; an 81-point exact-circuit grid has rank-2 Jacobians at seven probes and no coarse-grid duplicate. The approximate DEM differs from exact circuit means by at most 3.30e-5 on that grid. Off-grid/global aliases, finite-history accuracy and useful decoder-risk reduction remain unknown; the controller should not advance on these population calculations alone. Reproduction code/results and their hashes are in `SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md`.

An oracle generalized-moment calculation at that same DEM point gives only a modest theoretical efficiency difference: full single-shot covariance weighting lowers asymptotic standard deviation by about 7–8% relative to diagonal-variance weighting when the true covariance is known. Estimating and regularizing that covariance from finite detector histories could erase the gain; the simpler feature-standardized profile remains preferable absent a fair finite-history benefit.

**Decoder-family coverage update:** Analog/soft information and leakage-aware adaptation depend on raw readout likelihoods or explicit leakage/heralding signals absent from the current interface. Recurrent/transformer decoders learn logical predictions from syndrome histories with logical labels and substantial training/data requirements; they address a different output than a drift controller. Harmonized matching ensembles are the most relevant newly reviewed route to strengthen the *static* decoder comparator, but require a local version/API audit, equivalent model inputs, a frozen ensemble-selection budget, and runtime accounting before inclusion. Renormalization-group decoders remain possible alternatives with no identified Q-SHIFT-specific advantage. None displaces identifiability/model adequacy as the first gate.

**System-risk separation:** ReloQate adds a different response layer: infer a conservative logical-risk proxy from detector-fire-rate history and remap the logical patch. This demonstrates why a single detector-only alarm can have a valid operational purpose without identifying the underlying physical cause. Q-SHIFT's current research objective is narrower: determine burst/measurement identifiability and whether changing among cached decoder graphs improves unconditional logical risk. Do not merge the monitor, cause estimator, decoder policy and hardware remapping into one unexamined “adaptive decoder” claim.

**Prior-art update — learned syndrome-to-weights:** Chamberland et al. (2026 preprint) directly train from two-round syndrome data to estimate surface-code edge/hyperedge probabilities and construct standard/correlated PyMatching models. This is more directly adjacent than a generic classifier and makes “detector-only weight estimation” alone non-novel. Its 25-parameter rotated-surface-code formulas, large training/data/GPU recipe and static batch inference differ from Q-SHIFT's fixed known pair-burst scalar and causal switching hypothesis. Treat it as a key conceptual comparator if the project later expands to supervised batch weight estimation; do not assert the distinction is novel until a careful side-by-side contribution analysis is completed.


**Fixed gate-noise sensitivity:** a local exact-circuit three-parameter Jacobian (burst, measurement, scalar gate probability) has rank 3, but the gate derivative aligns strongly with the measurement direction (cosine 0.825) and 82.6% of its standardized norm lies in the burst/measurement span. This is a plausible nuisance-confounding direction for the existing fixed-gate burst-only estimator. It does not quantify finite-history bias and does not cover gate-channel composition drift. Keep gate calibration uncertainty in the next deterministic model-adequacy gate. Reproduction and assumptions are recorded in `SOURCE_VERIFICATION.md`.

**Window transfer and time axis:** published sliding-window formulas index syndrome-extraction cycles in an ongoing memory. Q-SHIFT's present evidence indexes independently reset completed shots, each with five internal rounds and a single shotwise burst parameter. An exact categorical population-mixture calculation across two rate regimes shows that pooled pair moments followed by the published inversion recover the arithmetic mean rate across these shots to `6.94e-17` maximum absolute error in 15 deterministic mixtures. This supports a trailing-window *mean-rate* candidate under static background assumptions. The relative-window population difference has no demonstrated finite-history utility at shot granularity: with one vector per shot, overlapping sample-average differencing leaves the single newest vector. Details and reproducer are in `DRIFT_WINDOW_TIME_AXIS_AUDIT.md` and `shot_window_mixture_audit.py`.

**Causal lag:** for a p step between independent shots, a trailing width-W population estimate is `p0+(n/W)(p1-p0)` after n post-change observations and only reaches p1 after W such observations. The first post-change shot cannot benefit from its own detector record because prediction must precede observation. EWMA has the step response `p1+(p0-p1)(1-λ)^n`. These are estimator lag formulas, not decoder-performance or policy thresholds; comparisons need equal prior information and development-only tuning.

**Mixed measurement drift sensitivity:** exact-circuit population mixtures with measurement p changing across shots produced edge-inversion burst-p bias of about `2.4–2.5e-5` when burst p was fixed, and up to `1.02e-4` in the two tested correlated burst/measurement mixtures. The approximate DEM matched these values closely in the tested conditions. This modest structural bias is below the existing burst-only profile mismatch in p units, but remains model-specific and does not provide finite-history or readout-correlation protection. See `shot_window_nuisance_mixture_audit.py`.

**Per-shot change information:** the exact joint ten-detector distribution has KL `.0348` nats/shot for a `.002→.010` burst step and `.01197` for `.010→.018` at measurement p `.003`; the coarse independently varying old/new measurement grid lowers these only to `.03367` and `.01172`. This supports nonzero model-based information per completed shot, but does not give an alarm threshold, delay, or false-alarm guarantee. A sequential trigger still needs finite-history calibration and a nuisance-robust stopping rule. See `burst_step_information_audit.py`.

**Finite-history estimator form:** exact one-pair count enumeration shows the raw moment inverse often returns a negative, physically inadmissible p in sparse-event windows. A bounded exact-model pair multinomial MLE avoids negative outputs, but puts substantial probability mass at zero and only modestly lowers single-pair RMSE at W=200 in the tested model. Keep both as deterministic candidates; a five-pair composite likelihood must account for within-shot dependence and needs a sandwich or joint finite-history uncertainty analysis. Do not regard the one-edge MLE as an established controller or use its exact-model advantage as evidence of decoder value. Reproduction in `exact_edge_multinomial_audit.py` and `exact_edge_likelihood_audit.py`.


**Burst-only population mismatch result:** with ideal exact-categorical templates fixed at measurement/gate p=0.003, an exploratory true measurement p shift to 0.001 or 0.005 moved fitted burst p by roughly −0.0019 or +0.0019 across three true burst settings. The profile retained a nonzero detector-feature residual, suggesting an OOD/mismatch diagnostic and conservative fallback are necessary before interpreting p_burst. This is not a sampled estimator test and no residual threshold is calibrated; finite-history false-alarm and decoder-risk consequences remain open.


**Scalar gate mismatch:** with true gate p at 0.002/0.004 and the burst-profile templates fixed at 0.003, the same population fit shifts burst p about −0.0020/+0.0020 to +0.0023 across the tested burst points. Residuals remain nonzero. The one-parameter policy therefore needs a detector-only adequacy guard that can attribute neither residual to a unique physical cause and falls back conservatively. No finite-history alarm threshold or logical-risk consequence is calibrated.


**Weighting sensitivity:** the idealized mismatch bias is similar under nominal exact-circuit SD and equal-weight pooled template SDs, but increases when the residual is unweighted. Source inspection confirms two 2,000-shot batches at each of three template knots, so equal-weight pooling has the same population-limit weights as the historical estimator. It does not reproduce the finite-sample scales estimated from those realized batches; therefore these population bias values still do not establish the realized historical estimator's behavior. Preserve standardized scaling and include a prespecified scale/mismatch sensitivity analysis in any future protocol.

### Literature update — hardware control vs decoder adaptation (checkpoint 95)

The July 2026 Willow RL paper materially strengthens external evidence that detector statistics can serve as feedback in an adaptive QEC system, but its main actuator is the physical control stack. Its separately reported decoder-prior steering uses logical-error-rate estimation, so it does not establish a scalable detector-only decoder switch. The Q-SHIFT novelty boundary is now narrower: a known-support, software-only burst-rate estimator and graph-selection policy on completed independent shots, evaluated against equally informed static and periodic policies. That contribution still requires demonstrated utility and a defined information/latency budget.

Mamba is a peer-reviewed low-latency supervised decoder architecture with experimental memory-data evaluation and simulated latency penalties; its training and per-shot prediction objective differ from Q-SHIFT's causal estimator. Variational TN noise learning optimizes a logical-label loss and remains a distinct high-information route. Do not merge these methods into one generic “adaptive decoder” evidence category.

### Structural actuator constraint (checkpoint 96)

The current Q-SHIFT software action changes an assumed decoder model used on completed detector arrays. It does not change the physical generator. A detector-rate objective can therefore identify noise or unsupported change, but cannot directly provide a reward gradient over decoder choices when detector data are policy-invariant. Keep hardware-control RL out of the decoder-only baseline matrix. See `ACTUATOR_INFORMATION_SEPARATION.md` for source paths and the conditional factorization.

### Checkpoint 102 — alarm-only e-detector candidate
The bounded cofire-mean screen has a nonasymptotic change-detection analogue in e-detectors: when a per-shot bounded (X_t\in[0,1]) obeys a uniform conditional null mean envelope (E[X_t|F_{t-1}]\le m), the increment (L_t=1+\lambda(X_t/m-1)), (0\le\lambda\le1), is nonnegative with conditional expectation at most one. The e-detector construction of Shin, Ramdas & Rinaldo then provides an ARL-valid threshold under its e-detector conditions. This is a useful candidate for robust *alarm* semantics under time-varying nuisance, potentially avoiding estimated pre-change likelihoods. Its exact Q-SHIFT prerequisite is not yet met: the pm supremum was checked on a 101-point grid, not certified continuously, and the null statistic bound must hold conditionally at every shot. The alarm would detect a cofire-mean excess only; parameter attribution and decoder graph switching need separate evidence. See `BOUNDED_MEAN_EPROCESS_FEASIBILITY.md`.

### Checkpoint 111 — e-factor ranking update
The exact-circuit null rectangle supports two simple detector-only e-factor forms: a scalar bounded-mean factor and a six-category cofire-count factor `q(C)/U_C`. Both are valid only under the stated conditional circuit model, bounded predictable nuisance range, and fixed gate-noise assumptions. The category construction uses more model-specific constants and has lower expected log growth than the fixed-λ mean factor on the declared alternative grid. Keep the scalar factor as the provisional primary trigger; retain category-wise betting as a secondary option if future nuisance modeling justifies it. Do not turn either process into graph updates until the owner chooses ARL versus finite-horizon false-switch risk, reset/alpha-spending policy, and a decoder-utility objective.

### Bidirectional dead-band trigger candidate
The exact continuous mean certificate can support separate upward and downward e-factors: upward uses $X$ under the low-burst null; downward uses $1-X$ under the high-burst null. The evidence rectangles leave the middle burst interval `(.002,.010)` unclassified. Keep the scalar mean factor as the preferred statistic because its sufficient conditional-mean certificates are simpler and its tested population log growth exceeds the current category proposal. A controller still needs a regime-state transition rule, hysteresis/dwell criteria, and false-switch/error spending across resets. The analytic lower bounds are not measured delays or decoder-benefit estimates.

### Checkpoint 117 — scalar-factor alternative drift certified
The scalar bounded-mean factor now has conservative continuous positive expected-log lower bounds for both one-sided regimes: `0.0107107` nats/shot upward and `3.6739e-5` downward at fixed `lambda=.005`, under the exact categorical circuit and bounded measurement nuisance. ### Gate-noise assumption is a hard validity boundary
The continuous scalar e-factor proofs fix gate p at `.003`. A deterministic exact-circuit 3x3x3 gate/measurement/burst grid shows that gate p=.004 can violate the low-regime mean null bound and gate p=.002 can violate the high-regime mean floor. Do not deploy the trigger under gate drift without a continuous robust gate envelope or a separately justified calibration guard. The current detector-only features have not been shown to attribute such a violation to gate noise rather than burst drift.

### Checkpoint 122 — robust gate interval without online gate attribution
The checkpoint-118 fixed-gate claim remains invalid under gate mismatch, but a new rational tensor-Bernstein certificate supplies a robust mean envelope over gate p `[.002,.004]` jointly with measurement p `[.001,.005]`. For low-burst null `[0,.002]`, `E[X] <= .003006472621297416`; for high-burst alternative `[.010,.018]`, `E[X] >= .009903071523335840`. The complementary `1-X` yields the same mean gap. A concavity-chord bound gives positive uniform expected-log drift for one-sided mean e-factors in both alternatives, including gate variation within that rectangle. This means detector-only gate attribution is not mathematically required for this narrower bounded nuisance family: the trigger can use the worst-case gate envelope. The guarantee requires the gate probability actually remain within `[.002,.004]`, the balanced exact categorical fault support, and fresh per-shot model conditionals. It does not solve alarm thresholds, delay, indifference-band action, resets, repeated-switch error spending, or graph utility. `exact_three_parameter_efactor_envelope.py` and its result document the continuous certificate; no sequential process or decoder switching was implemented.

The endpoint-chord lower-bound optimizer gives `lambda*=.0069174` upward and `.6964101` downward, maximizing the certified lower bound at `.00493254` and `.00333661` nats/shot. Because the downward factor has a large multiplicative range even though its certified drift is positive, keep `.005` as the conservative illustrative value and require future stability/resource/error-spending review before selecting any operational lambda. These calculations do not estimate detection delay.

### Checkpoint 125 — shot-axis role of the e-factor
The e-factor is a post-shot regime alarm, not a parameter estimate or a per-shot decoder selector. `X_t` is available only after completed shot t; any resulting graph choice affects shot t+1 or later. The current simulator's graph choice is downstream of detector generation, so a decoder-only action does not itself change the monitoring law. A physical-control action would require a separate closed-loop model and invalidate this conditional factorization unless rederived. Keep trailing-window/EWMA/periodic controls at the same shot-level information boundary; the relative-window inversion still collapses to the newest single vector.

### Checkpoint 126 — do not reset the opposite process after a possibly false switch
The upward and downward e-factor guarantees have different regime nulls. If a low-to-high alarm is false, a high-state downward process is not being run under its certified high-regime null, so alpha-spending by reset count does not by itself control the next false switch. The current mathematics supports one-sided sustained-null alarms; autonomous bidirectional switching needs a state-recovery/abstention rule, a reliable state initialization source or a joint robust policy theorem, plus an owner-selected false-switch risk target. Hold controller claims until this is solved.

### Checkpoint 131 — one-change mixture alarm candidate
A fixed-prior mixture over candidate abrupt change times can be updated in constant time and memory while retaining one-direction anytime null control. This is a mathematically specified alarm candidate under a trusted initial regime, not an adaptive decoder. Its later-change penalty is quantified in the e-process feasibility note. Repeated or reverse switches remain unsupported.

### Implementation constraint from checkpoint 133
If a future engineering study implements the one-change mixture, compute the six factor lookups from verified exact rational constants, use downward fixed-point rounding for the factor/state/prior masses, and compare against the alarm threshold using integers. The prototype invariant is `mixture_lower <= exact_mixture`; treat fixed-width overflow, t counter limits, constant serialization and any non-Python implementation as separate validation gates. This implementation detail does not promote the detector or authorize decoder switching.

### Checkpoint 138 — effect-size mixture architecture
A small fixed lambda bank can cover multiple certified burst regions while preserving a single directional null guarantee. Implement one constant-memory change-time mixture per fixed lambda and combine the four outputs with a fixed prior; do not estimate lambda online. This adds four detector-state updates and a `log(4)` evidence penalty. Use a trusted initial direction, future-shot-only action, and external baseline verification before any new episode. No controller or decoder switch was run.
