The exact five-pair cofire-mask polynomial construction has an independent algebraic check: its six count-category probabilities normalized identically, and a separate 1,024-state XOR probability recursion agreed at three deterministic parameter points. However, converting the weighted log-square polynomial to exact three-dimensional Bernstein coefficients did not finish within a bounded run (about 133.5 s process CPU and 440 MiB peak observed working set); the process was stopped before output. No continuous second-moment envelope or Freedman delay guarantee is available from this attempt. The broad-range Azuma sufficient bounds from checkpoint 128 remain the only certified finite-time crossing bounds.

### Checkpoint 130 partial resolution — continuous finite-time timing bound improved
A Jensen upper bound on the alternative expected log increment, combined with Bhatia-Davis conditional variance and Freedman concentration, yields markedly tighter sufficient crossing quantiles than Azuma using only the already certified continuous scalar-mean envelopes. The bound still assumes a fixed one-sided process and a sustained conditional alternative. Repeated switches, state recovery, deployment-scale arithmetic, indifference-band behavior and decoder utility remain open.

### Checkpoint 131 partial resolution — one unknown change time without restarting evidence
A fixed-prior mixture over candidate change starts yields a valid one-sided anytime alarm with an O(1) recurrence, avoiding repeated resets. Its false-alarm guarantee is global over time for one preselected direction and corresponding null. The prior creates a growing delay penalty for later changes, quantified in the new analytic table. This does not solve bidirectional switching, multiple changes, initial-regime uncertainty, or the indifference band.

### Checkpoint 132 partial resolution — finite-horizon prior tradeoff
A prespecified deployment horizon gives a uniform prior over all candidate change shots and improves the worst start-time penalty relative to the infinite telescoping prior. The no-change false-alarm bound remains time-uniform, but changes after H have no guaranteed component. Owner must define deployment horizon and whether any post-H action is allowed; no repeated-switch capability follows.

### Checkpoint 133 partial resolution — arithmetic conservatism prototype
A fixed-point integer recurrence with downward rounding preserves a pointwise lower bound on the exact mixture, so any alarm from that lower value is conservative under the exact e-process premise. The Python arbitrary-precision prototype is exhaustively checked on length-four abstract factor sequences, but production platform bounds and source-constant outward rounding remain open.

### Checkpoint 134 partial resolution — the indifference band has directional evidence subregions
The exact gate-robust categorical model certifies positive expected-log drift for an upward monitor in the top half of the prior burst indifference band and for a downward monitor in its bottom quarter. This narrows, but does not eliminate, the state-policy gap: middle regimes may produce alarms without a prevalidated intermediate graph action, and the remaining subbands have unclassified sign. Continuous smooth-drift paths are not bounded by the sustained-plateau results.

### Checkpoint 135 partial resolution — one specified smooth-ramp family
The e-mixture has a finite-time conditional detection bound for a concrete linear up/down burst ramp when it starts a candidate component at first entry to a sign-certified band and the burst rate then follows the specified ramp and endpoint hold. This narrows the prior “no smooth-drift statement” only for that path family. It does not cover arbitrary ramps, unknown dwell limits, or action utility; owner policy still needs to define what a mid-band alarm does.

### Checkpoint 136 owner decision — allowable reset authority
A mathematically controlled multi-episode design is possible only if every episode's initial regime and directional null are independently trusted; allocate a summable alpha budget across episodes. An alarm is not proof of the opposite regime. Decide whether external calibration/owner confirmation is permitted between episodes, whether one fixed direction per episode is acceptable, and what total false-switch budget is required. Automatic reset from the alarm remains unsupported.

### Checkpoint 137 fixed-point horizon limitation
The Q=2^96 lower-rounded infinite prior only represents candidate starts through s=281,474,976,710,655. Beyond that, weights floor to zero: alarms remain conservative, but the mixture no longer guarantees detection delay for arbitrarily late starts. A practical deployment should select a finite horizon and uniform prior, or use a different precision/representation with a separately proven cutoff.

### Checkpoint 138 partial resolution — effect-size coverage
A finite fixed lambda mixture closes much of the sign gap left by the endpoint-tuned scalar process: all four tested burst subregions per direction now have a positive continuous expected-log lower bound. The bank is mathematically valid only as a fixed convex mixture under the corresponding single null. Its larger state cost, bank weights, intermediate action, and lack of decoder utility remain open.
