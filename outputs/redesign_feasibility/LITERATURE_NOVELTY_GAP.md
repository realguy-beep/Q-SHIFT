# Literature and novelty gap

**Review date:** 6 November 2026. This is a targeted review of primary papers and official software documentation, not a systematic review or a novelty certification.

## What is already established

1. **Syndrome statistics can estimate decoder-relevant noise.** Spitz et al. derive matching-edge probabilities from syndrome correlations and use sliding windows to adapt weights in a time-dependent environment. Their paper explicitly identifies the window-size tradeoff: short windows are noisy; long windows lag the changing channel. It also says adaptation fails when the noise changes too quickly to collect enough data. [Spitz et al., *Advanced Quantum Technologies* (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1002/qute.201800012).
2. **Real-time error-rate learning predates the current proposal.** Huo and Li proposed estimating and predicting time-varying error rates from past QEC data using Gaussian processes, with decoder/use of those estimates intended to reduce logical failures. [Huo & Li, *New Journal of Physics* 19, 123032 (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/aa916e).
3. **The graph can be reweighted from decoding statistics.** DGR estimates edge probabilities and pairwise correlations from how often edges and edge pairs occur in MWPM solutions, then performs alignment and correlation reweighting. It studies drifted and correlated noise on surface and honeycomb codes. This directly overlaps with online matching-weight adaptation, although its estimator and update rule differ from a parameter posterior or syndrome-correlation estimator. [Wang et al., “DGR” (2023 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16214).
4. **Decoder priors can be calibrated against logical performance.** Sivak et al. use a reinforcement-learning-inspired method to calibrate decoder priors and report surface/repetition memory experiments on Sycamore. This precedes the proposed model update even though it is not the same detector-only drift-tracking policy. [Sivak et al., *Physical Review Letters* 133, 150603 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.150603).
5. **Time-varying syndrome-only estimation is now directly demonstrated in recent work.** Bhardwaj et al. present sliding-window estimation of time-dependent Pauli noise from QEC syndrome statistics, derive window/filter tradeoffs, and evaluate estimated-model decoding against static and ground-truth models under phenomenological and circuit-level simulations. This is very close to the proposed research objective. [Bhardwaj et al., *PRX Quantum* 7, 033024 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1hc-nqw5).
6. **Sequential inference is available for broader noise models.** Kobori and Todo use Bayesian MCMC for stationary parameters and sequential Monte Carlo for time-varying noise parameters inferred from surface-code syndrome statistics. The work includes non-Pauli models in simulation. [Kobori & Todo, *Physical Review A* 112, 052448 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1103/wg5h-spy6).
7. **Experimental matching calibration is established.** Remm et al. estimate contextual error mechanisms and matching weights from syndrome correlations in a distance-three surface-code experiment, including Y, hook, multiqubit and leakage-related events. [Remm et al., *Physical Review Research* 8, 013044 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/z1ng-wg3k). Earlier, Kelly et al. demonstrated in-situ calibration during repetitive error detection. [Kelly et al., *Physical Review A* 94, 032321 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.94.032321).
8. **Runtime QEC adaptation is not a new broad category.** Sivak et al. experimentally use error-detection events to train a reinforcement-learning controller for ongoing system control and decoder steering on a Willow processor. They report injected-drift and natural-drift results. Their method couples physical control and decoder adaptation; it is not identical to a software-only syndrome-to-MWPM-weight estimator, but it rules out claims that Q-SHIFT is the first adaptive QEC system. [Sivak et al., *Nature* 655, 879–884 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10759-2).
9. **Noise identifiability has mathematical limits and structure.** Wagner et al. prove Pauli-channel identifiability from stabilizer-code syndrome measurements for correlations up to the code's pure distance, with a data-syndrome treatment of measurement errors. This is not a guarantee that every multi-parameter circuit-level noise model is well-conditioned from finite detector windows. [Wagner et al., *Quantum* 6, 809 (2022)](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2022-09-19-809/).

PyMatching is a mature MWPM package. PyMatching v2's sparse blossom targets fast graph decoding; correlated matching is an additional two-pass procedure that relies on decomposable correlations. [Higgott & Gidney, “Sparse Blossom”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15933), [PyMatching API](https://pymatching.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api.html). Stim DEMs represent independent mechanisms; its documentation explains that some categorical Pauli channels require an approximation when converted into independent DEM mechanisms. [Stim DEM documentation](https://github.com/quantumlib/Stim/blob/main/doc/usage_command_line.md).

## What Q-SHIFT has and has not established

The repository's classifier-and-switch system has not found reliable decoder-utility headroom. The headroom audit covered 36 cells and 702 candidate pairs; 95 pairs were prediction-identical on finite evaluated samples, but their saved DEM/graph signatures were not identical. Fourteen specialist selections differed from pooled fixed; seven improved evaluation LER, six worsened it, and one tied. Only one cell passed its individual feasibility gate, with no qualifying pair repeated across two distances. These are development feasibility results, not a theorem against adaptation. The current code estimates a finite nine-model choice from detector marginals; it does not estimate continuous circuit error parameters or update matching weights online.

## Novelty judgment

**Online, causal, syndrome-based matching-weight adaptation is not itself a defensible novel claim.** It is directly anticipated by the 2018 adaptive estimator, the 2023 DGR method, the 2026 sliding-window work, Bayesian sequential estimation, and 2026 hardware adaptation.

A narrower potential contribution is a **budget-matched comparative study** asking whether continuously/event-triggered causal weight updates add value beyond (a) the strongest pooled fixed model and (b) periodic causal recalibration receiving the same syndrome history and update compute, across explicit drift timescales. The reviewed summaries of the 2026 sliding-window study emphasize estimated versus static and ground-truth models; this review has not established whether its full supplement already contains the exact equal-budget periodic comparison proposed here. That gap must be checked before any novelty statement. The contribution, if distinct, would be a controlled benchmark and operating-envelope result, not a new estimator.
