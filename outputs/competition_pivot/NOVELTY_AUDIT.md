# Novelty audit: uncertainty-gated decoder selection

**Scope:** candidate approach only. This review does not close Q-SHIFT or revisit frozen results. Literature was checked on 6 November 2026. Peer-reviewed papers and primary preprints are distinguished below.

## Closest prior work

| Prior work | What it already does | Relevance and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Sheth, Jafarzadeh & Gheorghiu, “Neural ensemble decoding for topological quantum error-correcting codes,” *Phys. Rev. A* **101**, 032338 (2020), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.101.032338), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02345), §§III.1–III.4, Algorithm 1, §IV | A syndrome classifier selects which of several decoders to apply to that syndrome. Training labels are formed by recording all decoders’ success/failure on the same simulated syndrome; samples where all succeed or all fail are discarded. The paper tests an MWPM/HDRG ensemble on surface-code depolarizing noise. | Direct prior art for observable-only selection among decoder outputs, including learning when a specialist should be used. It does not study Q-SHIFT’s detector-marginal calibration confidence, fixed-safe fallback, or drifting circuit-level gate/measurement mixture. |
| Smith, Brown & Bartlett, “Mitigating errors in logical qubits,” *Commun. Phys.* **7**, 386 (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-024-01883-4), §§ on exclusive decoders and complementary gap | Uses decoding ambiguity / complementary-gap information to reject difficult decoding instances, trading acceptance for a lower logical failure rate. | Establishes confidence-based selective acceptance and safe abstention as an existing QEC design pattern. It does not select between noise-specialist PyMatching outputs. |
| Mishima et al., “Adaptive Window Decoding based on Spatiotemporal Complementary Gap,” arXiv:2605.14637v2 (2026), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14637), §§III–IV, Appendices A–B | Starts with a cheaper/smaller window; if syndrome-derived confidence is low, enlarges the window and decodes again. The fallback is a more accurate, more expensive decoding configuration. | Closely matches confidence-gated escalation/fallback. The target is compute/latency versus LER, not selection among Q-SHIFT noise-model specialists. It is a preprint, not a peer-reviewed publication in the version inspected. |
| Oberoi, Viszlai & Chong, “ADaPT: Adaptive-window Decoding for Practical fault-Tolerance,” arXiv:2605.01149v1 (2026), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01149), §§III-B–III-C and IV | Uses cluster statistics as a confidence signal; low confidence triggers re-decoding with a larger window. A retry-rate controller adjusts the cutoff and uses a dead zone to damp oscillation. | Direct prior art for confidence-triggered decoder escalation, causal statistics, and hysteresis-like safeguards. It changes window resources rather than the physical noise model. Preprint status. |
| Choudhary et al., “MDQEC-QAS: Meta-Decoding for Quantum Error Correction with Hardware-Aware VQC Search and Confidence-Gated Recovery,” arXiv:2607.10707v1 (2026), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10707), §3.5, §§4.8–4.9, Table 3 | Learns syndrome-to-recovery mappings and gates learned recovery with confidence, routing uncertain cases to a conventional teacher decoder. The authors report the greatest benefit in a difficult Planar5×5 transfer condition. | Direct conceptual overlap with an adaptive decoder plus trusted fallback. The evaluated method is a learned/meta-decoder, not Q-SHIFT’s calibrated matching bank. It is an unreviewed preprint and should not be treated as settled evidence. |
| Sheth et al. (above) and Dang, “Adaptive decoder selection for quantum LDPC codes is effectively one-dimensional,” Zenodo [record 21466148](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21466148) (2026), main paper | The latter analyzes syndrome-conditioned selection among decoders with an accuracy/latency trade-off and reports that a simple threshold can suffice in its studied QLDPC setting. | Selection is established; the choice of confidence feature and cost model matters. The 2026 record is an artifact/preprint-style publication and is not peer-reviewed evidence of universal behavior. |

Decoder soft outputs also provide directly observable reliability scores. Lee, English & Bartlett, “Efficient post-selection for general quantum LDPC Codes,” *npj Quantum Information* **12**, 96 (2026), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-026-01242-x), §§2.1–2.6 and 2.9–3, use correction likelihoods, comparative logical gaps, detector density, and cluster-derived confidence for post-selection. Their strategies reject/abort uncertain samples rather than select a different noise-specialist decoder. The article explicitly notes that detector density and correction weight can be weaker confidence proxies than cluster geometry.

## Audit conclusion

The broad idea “use syndrome-visible confidence to decide whether to trust an adaptive decoder or fall back to a fixed decoder” is not novel. It is a close combination of (a) per-syndrome ensemble decoder selection, (b) confidence-based rejection, and (c) adaptive weak-to-strong decoder fallback. A narrower claim might concern **calibration uncertainty predicting incremental logical utility of a noise-specialist matching decoder under drift**, but Q-SHIFT’s available evidence does not establish that this signal is predictive or useful. The pilot below gives negative or inconclusive results.

## Limits of this review

The 2026 arXiv works are preprints, not confirmation that their results are independently replicated. Their existence still prevents a broad novelty claim. The literature review is focused on directly relevant decoder selection, confidence, and fallback methods; it is not an exhaustive review of all classical selective prediction or QEC soft-decision literature. No claim is made that the exact “calibration-score margin to per-block utility” mapping has already been tested in an identical circuit and noise setting.
